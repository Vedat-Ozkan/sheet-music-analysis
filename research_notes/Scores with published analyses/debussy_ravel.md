# Debussy and Ravel: pieces with both a published human analysis and a free MusicXML score

Researched 2 October 2026. Evidence labels used throughout:

- **[read]** = I downloaded the document and read the text myself.
- **[relayed]** = the page was fetched through a summarising fetch tool; its "quotes" were relayed by that tool and I did not see the page text directly. Treat as unconfirmed wording.
- **[not opened]** = found by search only; the page or PDF would not load. Nothing about its content is confirmed.
- **[checked]** = score file downloaded with curl and parsed (zip/XML well-formed, bar count, staves, metadata).

Headline: Debussy has four solid candidates. Ravel has only two candidates whose analysis I read myself (Sonatine I, Jeux d'eau); the Pavane has the best score file but only thin analyses. The brief asked for at least four solid Ravel candidates; that was not achieved.

## Which popular Debussy and Ravel pieces have a thorough human-written analysis available?

### Takeaway
Debussy: "La fille aux cheveux de lin", "Des pas sur la neige", "Voiles" and "La cathédrale engloutie" each have a free, whole-piece, bar-numbered analysis. Ravel: Sonatine (first movement) and "Jeux d'eau" have free thesis-level analyses, but neither is chord-by-chord; for the Pavane, Menuet sur le nom d'Haydn and the String Quartet I found only thin or unopened material.

### Cited Findings
- "La fille aux cheveux de lin": bar-by-bar harmonic analysis of all 39 bars by Gonzalo Varela (teaching-assistant marking key, Universidad de la República, Montevideo), covering chords, Roman numerals, pentatonic and hexatonic fields, parallelism, cadence types and modulations. **[read]** — [Varela PDF](https://gonzalovarela.com/assets/doc/studies/An%C3%A1lisis%20arm%C3%B3nico%20de%20'La%20fille%20aux%20cheveux%20de%20lin'%20(Preludio%20Libro%201%20No.%208)%20de%20Claude%20Debussy-EN.pdf)
- "La fille aux cheveux de lin": two further analyses exist, a topic-by-topic one on teoria.com (2001) and a Schenkerian one in Music Theory Spectrum (2009). **[read]** — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/debussy-fille-aux-cheveux-de-lin/index.php); [Day-O'Connell PDF](https://www.skidmore.edu/music/documents/Day-OConnell_Debussy_Pentatonicism.pdf)
- "Des pas sur la neige": whole-piece analysis in five bar-range pages (first measures, 5–11, 12–19, 20–25, 26–end) plus summary pages, with a score image per range; names chords, extensions, modes and the whole-tone passage. **[read]** — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/debussy-des-pas-sur-la-neige/index.php)
- "Voiles" and "La cathédrale engloutie": both are analysed whole, with form diagrams by bar number, in Melanie Loyd's 2017 M.M. thesis (University of Missouri–Columbia). Emphasis is on pitch collections, pedal, planing and thematic synthesis, not chord labels. **[read]** — [MOspace record](https://mospace.umsystem.edu/xmlui/handle/10355/62058); [thesis PDF](https://mospace.umsystem.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/b2825388-3053-43fa-81f8-15f8c91e278b/content)
- "Voiles": David Code's Scottish Music Review article (2007) is a critical and performance-centred reading, not a harmonic walk-through; I read only pages 1–3 of 25. **[read, partial]** — [copy on a third-party site](http://vickyjohnson.altervista.org/Analysis_Debussy_Voiles.pdf)
- "Clair de lune": a complete chord-label file exists (DCML labels converted to RomanText in When in Rome), bar 1 to the end; this is a label dataset, not a teacher-style analysis. **[read, first 24 bars]** — [When in Rome analysis.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Debussy,_Claude/Suite_Bergamasque,_L.75/3_Clair/analysis.txt)
- "Clair de lune": a 2010 blog analysis by Thomas Stone (about 2,500 words, bar references across bars 1–72, form, some chord symbols; no credentials stated). **[relayed]** — [thomasstone1.wordpress.com](https://thomasstone1.wordpress.com/2010/02/22/an-analysis-of-clair-de-lune-from-suite-bergamasque/)
- Open Music Theory does not analyse a Debussy piece in the chapters I fetched; "Voiles" is mentioned once as an illustration of the whole-tone collection. **[read]** — [OMT, Analyzing with Modes, Scales, and Collections](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/analyzing-with-collections-scales-and-modes/)
- "Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom" gives short excerpts only (e.g. "Clair de lune" bar 15, Pavane bar 27, Faune), not whole-piece analyses. **[relayed]** — [Puget Sound, 32.1 Impressionism](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/Impressionism.html)
- Ravel Sonatine, first movement: Robert Hjelmstad's 2019 D.M.A. thesis (University of Colorado, advisor Daphne Leong) gives the form with bar numbers and key areas, then a motivic analysis with 13 music examples for movement I; movements II and III are treated briefly. **[read]** — [Hjelmstad PDF](https://scholar.colorado.edu/downloads/xg94hp86p)
- Ravel "Jeux d'eau": Ting-Yu Liu's 2023 D.M.A. lecture-document (University of Oregon) has a section on the piece (document pp. 20–28) naming themes, chords, pentatonic, whole-tone and octatonic material at selected bars, with ten figures. **[read]** — [Liu PDF](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/1c78b6ba-97fe-4d56-90fb-15995c420e7b/content)
- Ravel "Jeux d'eau": Emily Dewoolfson's 2018 M.A. thesis in music theory (Penn State, advisor Eric McKee) analyses it as a sonata form with "modified Schenkerian tools", per its abstract. Abstract **[read]**; PDF **[not opened]** (connection reset, then 403). — [Penn State ETDA record](https://etda.libraries.psu.edu/catalog/15275exd42)
- Ravel Pavane: a student presentation outline (Sarah Rice, 2013) gives form A B A C A with bar ranges, phrase lengths, keys and modes. **[relayed]** — [Prezi](https://prezi.com/ggmelzxludtz/pavane-pour-une-infante-defunte/)
- Ravel Pavane: Kathryn Louderback's 2019 blog post (about 2,000 words, score images, G major / G Dorian / C Dorian) lacks consistent bar numbers; the fetch tool itself rated it "somewhat thin". **[relayed]** — [pianistmusings.com](https://pianistmusings.com/2019/01/11/a-look-inside-pavane-for-a-dead-princess/)
- Ravel Pavane: Gianmaria Griglio's article concerns the orchestral version and orchestration; no harmonic analysis. **[relayed]** — [gianmariagriglio.com](https://gianmariagriglio.com/conducting-ravel-pavane-pour-une-infante-defunte/)
- Ravel Pavane: a UNT thesis, "A Stylistic Analysis of the Piano Works of Debussy and Ravel" (xii, 178 leaves), gives only the key scheme on p. 140. I read that page's OCR; author and date were not retrieved. **[read, one page]** — [UNT Digital Library, p. 140](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc699677/m1/151/)
- Ravel Menuet sur le nom d'Haydn: a 2010 blog post by "Ethan" (about 550 words, no credentials, no score images) with Roman numerals at a few bars. Thin. **[relayed]** — [observationsmostlymusic.blogspot.com](http://observationsmostlymusic.blogspot.com/2010/12/analysis-of-ravels-menuet-sur-le-nom.html)
- Ravel Le Tombeau de Couperin: Ruth Henderson's 1947 master's document covers all six movements in 19 pages. Thin; I read only the title page and contents. **[read, partial]** — [UNT PDF](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc699552/m2/1/high_res_d/1002503126-Henderson.pdf)

### Inferences
- The strongest match to "the way a teacher or textbook might do" is Varela on "La fille": it was written to mark harmony homework, proceeds bar range by bar range, and takes positions on ambiguous readings.
- The Loyd thesis suits testing named regions (whole-tone, pentatonic, pedal, planing, form) but gives the tool nothing to compare chord or Roman-numeral labels against.
- Hjelmstad is the reverse case for Ravel: form, key areas and half-cadence-like arrivals are stated with bar numbers, but the bulk is motivic (set-class [0,2,5], interlocking fourths), so it checks form and tonal centres more than chords.
- Liu's "Jeux d'eau" section is selective (about 14 bar references across an 80-plus-bar piece), so it can confirm specific events but not a continuous analysis.

- "Clair de lune": the page titled "Complete Analysis – Clair de Lune by Debussy" ([link](https://www.piano-composer-teacher-london.co.uk/clair-de-lune-by-debussy-complete-analysis-2/)) returned 403 on every attempt. **Not opened.**
- Arabesque No. 1: search returned only student pages, a ResearchGate item and blogs; none was opened. No verified analysis.

### Takeaway
Six analyses were read directly and are quotable; the rest are relayed or unopened. Only Varela and the two teoria.com articles go through the music in bar order with harmony named at each step.

**1. Varela, "Harmonic Analysis of Claude Debussy's 'La fille aux cheveux de lin'" [read, text pages 1–4]** — [PDF](https://gonzalovarela.com/assets/doc/studies/An%C3%A1lisis%20arm%C3%B3nico%20de%20'La%20fille%20aux%20cheveux%20de%20lin'%20(Preludio%20Libro%201%20No.%208)%20de%20Claude%20Debussy-EN.pdf)

- Length: 6 PDF pages; 4 pages of text in 18 bar-range entries from "Mm1 - 2" to "Mm35 - End".
- Covers: chords and Roman numerals, pentatonic and hexatonic pitch fields, exact and diatonic parallelism, cadence types (plagal, authentic, deceptive), modulations (Gb, Eb, Cb), pedal, prolongation. Does not cover: phrase structure as such, overall form labels.

- Quote: "the progression Cb-Gb found on measures 2 and 3 is heard as a plagal progression IV-I (and because of the melodic rest we can consider it a plagal cadence)."
- Quote: "Mm8 - 9 (second beat): The initial melody is repeated, but this time harmonized with chords in exact parallelism, using major minor seventh chords in first inversion."
- Quote: "Mm15 - 16: ... the piece modulates to Cb major through a common chord, and this modulation is confirmed through a perfect authentic cadence, where the V ... again lacks the leading note, and takes the form of a quartal pentad (V7.9.11-no3-)."

**2. Rodríguez Alvira, "Harmonic Analysis of Debussy's Prelude La fille aux cheveux de lin" [read]** — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/debussy-fille-aux-cheveux-de-lin/index.php)
- Author: José Rodríguez Alvira; "© 2001 José Rodríguez Alvira. Published by teoria.com". Credentials not stated on the pages I read.
- Length: five short topical pages (non-traditional scales, extended harmony, traditional chords in non-traditional context, harmonic color, tonal ambiguity) plus a recording page.
- Covers: selected bars by topic with score images (page 4 has four, for bars 1–3, 8–10, 24–25, 28–30 **[relayed]**). Not a continuous bar-by-bar reading; no form or phrase analysis.
- Quote: "Measure 9, Db7 with 9th and 11th: This chord has a dominant of Gb function."

- Quote: "the piece constantly changes from Gb major to an Eb pentatonic/minor natural."

**3. Day-O'Connell, "Debussy, Pentatonicism, and the Tonal Tradition" [read, pp. 225–226 and example captions]** — [PDF on skidmore.edu](https://www.skidmore.edu/music/documents/Day-OConnell_Debussy_Pentatonicism.pdf)

- Covers: history of pentatonicism, the "plagal leading-tone", then a Schenkerian analysis of "La fille". Examples 28–36 are on "La fille" (beginning with analysis; "Formal and paradigmatic analysis"; measures 12–16; 18–22; 22–24; "linear analysis"; end).
- Quote (abstract): "through a Schenkerian analysis of La fille aux cheveux de lin, I reveal Debussy's innovative and far-reaching reformulation of structural norms in response to those traditions."

### Inferences
- None of the analyses read shows signs of being AI-generated: all are dated 2001–2023 with named human authors, and four are institutional theses or a journal article. Several search hits that I did not use (jeanmichelserres.com 2025 "Notes on ..." pages, grokipedia.com, pianio.blog) looked machine-written from their titles and were excluded without being opened.
- Bar numbering in the analyses agrees with the score files where I could compare: Loyd's last bars (64 and 89) equal the file bar counts for "Voiles" and "Cathédrale"; Rice's 2/4 bars 19, 49, 59 and final bar 72 match the ASAP Pavane file.

### Takeaway

**Specific defects found**
- Sonatine I: the TPC file contains no repeat or ending marks; file bars 29–50 are note-for-note identical to file bars 4–25, and file bars 26–28 are the first ending. The first theme's return sits at file bar 81, against Hjelmstad's "recapitulation from m. 56". So for analysis bar N of 26 or more, file bar = N + 25. Measured from the [TPC file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Ravel%2C%20Maurice%20(1875%20-%201937)/xml/Sonatina/Sonatine1.xml) against the [Hjelmstad thesis](https://scholar.colorado.edu/downloads/xg94hp86p).
- Two ABRSM-derived files in another repository look wrong and should not be used: "Voiles" with 77 measures and a five-flat signature, and "Pavane de la Belle au bois dormant" with 43 measures. — [ArturoBarrios/MusicClassification](https://github.com/ArturoBarrios/MusicClassification)
- The TPC folder name gives Debussy's dates as "(1862 - 1914)". — [repo tree](https://github.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus)

### Inferences
- The TPC is convenient (plain MusicXML for exactly the popular pieces) but is the least trustworthy source here: unlicensed, weeks-old at time of upload, single author, wrong composer dates, an unrolled repeat, a file labelled "excerpt", and a Pavane whose metre disagrees with ASAP. Where a DCML `.mscx` exists for the same piece it is probably the safer text, at the cost of a conversion step and a non-commercial licence.
- No licence on a repository means default copyright on the engraving even though the music is public domain; using TPC files in tests is low-risk, redistributing them in the plugin is not clearly permitted.
- Public-domain status: all candidate pieces were published by 1911 according to common knowledge, which would put them in the US public domain; Ravel died in 1937 and Debussy in 1918. I did not check any of this against a source in this session, nor the special French wartime extensions for Ravel. Flag for the owner if French distribution matters.

### Gaps
- Menuet sur le nom d'Haydn: the file has 55 bars, the blog analysis says 54. Not resolved; no printed score was consulted.
- "Jeux d'eau": the files have 88 measures, while Liu's highest bar reference is m. 84 for "the last few measures". Whether numbering differs around the cadenza was not checked.
- DCML `.mscx` to MusicXML conversion was not tested.
- The official ATEPP archive was not downloaded; the mirror's files are assumed, not confirmed, to be identical.
- No MusicXML was found for: Tombeau de Couperin "Menuet" (only the Toccata, in ATEPP), Ma mère l'Oye (only the suspect ABRSM file), Boléro (not searched beyond file names), Faune.
- GitHub filename search does not index binary `.mxl` files and hit the rate limit partway, so other repositories may exist.
- Score note content was not proof-read against a printed edition for any file.

## Which candidates are the strongest overall, ranked, and why?

### Takeaway
Debussy: 1 "La fille aux cheveux de lin", 2 "Des pas sur la neige", 3 "Voiles", 4 "La cathédrale engloutie". Ravel: 1 Sonatine I, 2 "Jeux d'eau", then the Pavane only if the owner accepts a thin analysis or supplies a textbook source; four solid Ravel candidates were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Debussy 1, "La fille aux cheveux de lin": the only piece with a continuous bar-range analysis giving chords, Roman numerals, scales, parallelism and cadences, plus two independent analyses to cross-check; score verified at 39 bars. — [Varela](https://gonzalovarela.com/assets/doc/studies/An%C3%A1lisis%20arm%C3%B3nico%20de%20'La%20fille%20aux%20cheveux%20de%20lin'%20(Preludio%20Libro%201%20No.%208)%20de%20Claude%20Debussy-EN.pdf); [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/debussy-fille-aux-cheveux-de-lin/index.php); [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Debussy%2C%20Claude%20(1862%20-%201914)/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Book1/8%20La%20fille%20aux%20cheveux%20de%20lin.xml)
- Debussy 2, "Des pas sur la neige": whole piece in bar order with chords, extensions, named modes and a whole-tone passage; 36 bars, short enough to check by hand. — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/debussy-des-pas-sur-la-neige/index.php); [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Debussy%2C%20Claude%20(1862%20-%201914)/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Book1/6%20Des%20pas%20sur%20la%20neige.xml)
- Debussy 3, "Voiles": exact region boundaries (whole-tone 1–41, pentatonic 42–47, whole-tone 48–64) and a B-flat pedal, a clean test of region naming; score verified at 64 bars but via a mirror. — [Loyd](https://mospace.umsystem.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/b2825388-3053-43fa-81f8-15f8c91e278b/content); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/esthy13/memuk/main/melody/partitions/ATEPP-1.1/raw/Claude_Debussy/Pr%C3%A9ludes%2C_Book_1%2C_L._117/No._2%2C_Voiles/Debussy_-_Voiles.mxl)
- Debussy 4, "La cathédrale engloutie": arch form with bar ranges, pentatonic collections, planing in bars 28–39, C pedal; score verified at 89 bars. — [Loyd](https://mospace.umsystem.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/b2825388-3053-43fa-81f8-15f8c91e278b/content); [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Debussy%2C%20Claude%20(1862%20-%201914)/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Book1/10%20La%20Cath%C3%A9drale%20engloutie.xml)
- Debussy 5, "Clair de lune": best score supply and a full chord-label file, but no teacher-style analysis was read. — [When in Rome labels](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Debussy,_Claude/Suite_Bergamasque,_L.75/3_Clair/analysis.txt); [MuseTrainer score](https://musetrainer.github.io/library/scores/Clair_de_Lune__Debussy.mxl)
- Debussy 6, Arabesque No. 1: score verified (107 bars), no analysis verified. — [MuseTrainer score](https://musetrainer.github.io/library/scores/Arabesque_L._66_No._1_in_E_Major.mxl)
- Ravel 1, Sonatine I: doctoral analysis with form, key areas and bar numbers; score complete but needs the bar-offset mapping above. — [Hjelmstad](https://scholar.colorado.edu/downloads/xg94hp86p); [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Ravel%2C%20Maurice%20(1875%20-%201937)/xml/Sonatina/Sonatine1.xml)
- Ravel 2, "Jeux d'eau": analysis names pentatonic, whole-tone, octatonic and polytonal events at specific bars, but selectively; the score is large (4,740 notes, three staves). — [Liu](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/1c78b6ba-97fe-4d56-90fb-15995c420e7b/content); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/esthy13/memuk/main/melody/partitions/ATEPP-1.1/raw/Maurice_Ravel/Jeux_d'eau%2C_M._30/musicxml_cleaned.musicxml)
- Ravel 3, Pavane: the most reliable score file of all (ASAP, 72 bars, metre changes agree with the outline), but analyses are an outline, a blog and a one-line key scheme. — [ASAP score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Ravel/Pavane/xml_score.musicxml); [Prezi outline](https://prezi.com/ggmelzxludtz/pavane-pour-une-infante-defunte/)

- Ravel 5, String Quartet: CC0 score verified; no analysis opened. — [OpenScore score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/StringQuartets/main/scores/Ravel%2C_Maurice/String_Quartet_in_F_major/sq8482283.mxl)

### Inferences
- A sensible Debussy pair is "La fille" (chords, Roman numerals, cadences, parallel sevenths) plus "Voiles" (pure region naming and pedal), since they test different parts of the tool; "Des pas sur la neige" is the alternative to "Voiles" if modes and chord extensions matter more than whole-tone regions.
- For Ravel, Sonatine I plus the Pavane would be the most useful pair musically (sonata form with modal harmony; rondo with parallel ninths and Dorian episodes), but the Pavane needs a better analysis first. The cheapest way to close that gap is probably a textbook or the two unopened theses, checked by a person with library access.
- "Jeux d'eau" is the stronger-documented second Ravel piece today, but its size and three-staff texture make it a hard first test.

### Gaps
- Ranking for Ravel 3–5 rests on relayed or unopened analyses and could change once the Penn State honors thesis, Dewoolfson, Sutarjo and Gooch are actually read.
- No analysis here was compared against the tool's output, and none of the score files was run through the engine; "strongest" is judged on documentation and file checks only.
- Whether the owner counts the Tonal Piano Corpus's missing licence as acceptable for internal testing is a decision I could not make.
