# Chopin, Schumann and Mendelssohn: popular pieces with a published human analysis AND a free MusicXML score

Research date: 2 October 2026. Everything marked "verified" was opened or downloaded in this session (pages fetched with curl and read as text; PDFs downloaded and text-extracted or viewed; score files downloaded and inspected for zip/XML structure, measure count, key and time signature). Items I could not open are listed under Gaps in each section.

Short names used below for score sources:

- **TPC** = Tonal Piano Corpus, github.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus (new find; Finale-engraved scores exported to MusicXML, from printed editions; created May 2026).
- **DCML-xml** = github.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml (new find; MusicXML conversions of the DCML MuseScore corpora, so no MuseScore conversion step is needed).
- **CFE-xml** = MusicXML conversions of the Chopin Institute's "Chopin First Editions" Humdrum scores, found inside github.com/nereasastre/Music_Corpus_Annotator.
- **WiR** = When in Rome corpus. **Lieder** = OpenScore Lieder corpus. **MuseTrainer** = github.com/musetrainer/library.

## Which popular pieces by these three composers have a thorough human-written analysis available?

### Takeaway
Seven Chopin candidates, six Schumann candidates and three Mendelssohn candidates have both an analysis and a score file; the ones with a genuinely thorough, bar-referenced, teacher-style analysis that is free online are Chopin Preludes Op. 28 Nos. 20, 4 and 6 (teoria.com annotated scores plus a 298-leaf thesis on all 24 preludes), Chopin Nocturne in E minor Op. 72 No. 1 (AQA exam-board teaching guide), Schumann Kinderszenen No. 1 (two independent analyses, one with an annotated outline) and Nos. 3 and 11 (Pearson Edexcel notes), and Mendelssohn Song Without Words Op. 19 No. 1 (a 2021 PhD dissertation with a complete Roman-numeral annotated score). For the most famous titles on the wish list (Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2, Träumerei, "Im wunderschönen Monat Mai", Venetian Boat Song Op. 30 No. 6, Spring Song) the free analyses I found are still descriptive or thin; the thorough ones are in books or paywalled.

### Cited Findings

**Chopin candidates (7 with analysis + score, plus supplements)**

- Prelude Op. 28 No. 20 in C minor: teoria.com has a 3-page analysis with Roman numerals printed under every chord of the score for bars 1–4 and 5–8 (the last phrase repeats 5–8), covering the modulation to A-flat, half cadence, passing diminished seventh, augmented sixth and Neapolitan — [teoria.com, Prelude No. 20](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2018/chopin-preludes/20/index.php)
- Prelude Op. 28 No. 4 in E minor: teoria.com has a 5-page analysis (first measures, voice movement bars 1–8, harmonic resources, measures 13–25, complete prelude), marked "Peer reviewed by MERLOT", © 2001 — [teoria.com, Prelude #4](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/chopin-prelude-04/index.php)
- Prelude Op. 28 No. 6 in B minor: teoria.com has a single-page analysis whose two score images cover the whole piece (bars 1–8 and 9–end) with chord symbols above and Roman numerals below the staff — [teoria.com, Prelude No. 6](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2019/chopin-preludes/06/index.php)
- Prelude Op. 28 No. 15 in D-flat ("Raindrop"): teoria.com gives a form table (compound ternary, with bar ranges and keys for every subsection) and a score excerpt for each subsection — [teoria.com, Prelude No. 15](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2019/chopin-preludes/15/index.php)
- Prelude Op. 28 No. 7 in A major: teoria.com has a short page (motif, two phrases, non-harmonic notes implying 7ths/9ths/13ths, dominant ninth) with one score image and a video — [teoria.com, Prelude No. 7](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2019/chopin-preludes/07/index.php)
- All 24 preludes are analysed on teoria.com (the collection index links to a page for each number) — [teoria.com, Chopin's Preludes collection](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2018/chopin-preludes/)
- All 24 preludes are also analysed, one by one, in a 1970 Master of Music thesis (298 leaves) covering form, harmony, key schemes and non-harmonic tones, with form diagrams and key-scheme tables in an appendix — [Daniel, "The Twenty-Four Preludes of Chopin, Opus 28: Formal Structure, Harmonic Deviations, and Modulation Devices", UNT Digital Library](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc663297/)
- Nocturne in E minor Op. 72 No. 1: the AQA A-level teaching guide walks through all 57 bars (phrases, cadences, keys, a chord-by-chord table for bars 14–22, suspensions and appoggiaturas, structure summary) — [AQA Teaching guide: Area of study 1, piano music of Chopin, Brahms and Grieg (PDF)](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/music/AQA-7272-TG-AOS1-CBG.PDF)
- Ballade No. 2 in F Op. 38 is analysed in the same AQA guide (from page 11), with a bar-range table of phrases and keys — [AQA Teaching guide (PDF)](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/music/AQA-7272-TG-AOS1-CBG.PDF)
- Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2: the best free page found is a teacher's form-and-teaching article (form map A–A′–B–A″–B′–A‴–Coda with three diagram images) that explicitly does not give chord-by-chord harmony — [Paperless Piano Teacher, "Chopin Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2: analysis and teaching tips"](https://paperlesspianoteacher.com/blog/chopin-nocturne-op-9-no-2-analysis)
- Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2: the standard scholarly analysis is John Rink's chapter, which says earlier analyses by Schenker and Salzer "fail to explain certain idiosyncratic aspects" (paywalled book chapter) — ["'Structural momentum' and closure in Chopin's Nocturne Op. 9, No. 2", Schenker Studies 2, Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/schenker-studies-2/structural-momentum-and-closure-in-chopins-nocturne-op-9-no-2/62A040B19DE1E60948923A19C67FB7D8)
- Mazurkas: the Polish Music Journal article treats phrase syntax and period form across the mazurkas published in Chopin's lifetime rather than analysing one piece from start to finish — [Witkowska-Zaremba, "Versification, Syntax and Form in Chopin's Mazurkas"](https://polishmusic.usc.edu/research/publications/polish-music-journal/vol3no1/chopin-mazurkas/)
- Supplement (bare chord labels): WiR has a human Roman-numeral file for Prelude No. 20 ("Analyst: Mark Gotham, manually, through judicious automation, and in collaboration with colleagues and students") — [WiR, Prelude Op. 28 No. 20 analysis.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Chopin%2C_Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric/Preludes%2C_Op.28/20/analysis.txt)
- Supplement: the DCML mazurka corpus has expert harmony labels for the mazurkas (e.g. Op. 68 No. 4 as `harmonies/BI168op68-4.tsv`), and WiR mirrors them — [WiR remote.json for Mazurka Op. 68 No. 4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Chopin%2C_Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric/Mazurkas/BI168/remote.json)

**Schumann candidates (6)**

- Kinderszenen Op. 15 Nos. 1, 3 and 11: official Pearson Edexcel set-work notes (7 pages) give, for each piece, Structure, Tonality, Harmony, Melody, Texture and Rhythm with bar-and-beat references — [Pearson Edexcel, "23. Schumann Kinderscenen, Op. 15: Nos 1, 3 and 11" (PDF)](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Music/2013/Teaching%20and%20learning%20materials/UNIT_3_-_23__Schumann_-_Kinderscenen_Op__15_-_Nos__1_3_and_11_June_2017.pdf)
- Kinderszenen No. 1 ("Von fremden Ländern und Menschen"): a second, independent whole-piece analysis in three web pages (formal analysis; voice leading and root progressions; phrase structure) plus a 2-page analytical outline PDF with Roman numerals, figured bass, passing/auxiliary/suspension labels and phrase trees — [Sutcliffe, "Schumann Formal Analysis"](https://www.harmony.org.uk/book/schumann_analysis/schumann_formal_analysis.htm) and [Analytical Outline PDF](https://www.harmony.org.uk/book/schumann_analysis/schumann_outline.pdf)
- Kinderszenen No. 7 ("Träumerei"): the fullest free prose analysis is an excerpt from Rob Kapilow's book "What Makes It Great" (Wiley, 2011, pp. 283–295), about 3,500–4,000 words with annotated score images for bars 1–24; it names keys (G minor at bar 11, B-flat major at bar 13) but not chords — [Mara Marietta, "Schumann – Kinderszenen – Analysis 1"](https://www.maramarietta.com/schumann-kinderszenen-analysis-1-traumerei/)
- Träumerei: a harmonic-function analysis exists only as a video (fallback), where "the harmonic functions with brackets are secondary dominants" — [Timon de Nood video, via "Harmonic Analysis: Träumerei (Op.15 No. 7)"](https://createmusic.wordpress.com/2019/10/18/harmonic-analysis-traumerei-op-15-no-7-r-schumann/)
- "Ich grolle nicht" (Dichterliebe No. 7): a per-song analysis names the chord in almost every bar ("C7, F7, a7, d7, F7, b7, d7, G7 ...") with measure numbers, but its author describes himself as an amateur — [Woltjer, Analysis of Schumann's Dichterliebe, song 7](https://geertwoltjer.nl/Songs/DichterliebeAnalysis/Dichterliebe7.html)
- "Im wunderschönen Monat Mai" (Dichterliebe No. 1): the same site has a short harmonic reading (b–C♯7 as iv–V7 of an F♯ minor that never arrives; II6–V7–I in A; IV–V7–I in B minor and D) — [Woltjer, song 1](https://geertwoltjer.nl/Songs/DichterliebeAnalysis/Dichterliebe1.html)
- "Im wunderschönen Monat Mai": a 2023 peer-reviewed article with eight annotated examples discusses tonal pairing and an allusion to Clara Schumann's concerto; it is interpretive scholarship rather than a chord-by-chord teaching analysis — [Keep, "The Voice of Enigma: Intertextuality in 'Im wunderschönen Monat Mai'", Intégral 36 (2023)](https://theory.esm.rochester.edu/integral/36-2023/keep/)
- "Im wunderschönen Monat Mai": a 2010 blog post walks through the first bars in student terms (iv6 with 9–8 suspension, C♯7, then the turn to A major) — [Musical Analysis for Singers](http://musicalanalysisforsingers.blogspot.com/2010/07/dichterliebe-im-wunderschonen-monat-mai.html)
- A 1957 master's thesis treats all 16 Dichterliebe songs but states "It is not the aim of this work to offer an extensive formal or harmonic analysis of this song cycle" — [Davidson, "Dichterliebe by Robert Schumann", UNT](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc699545/m2/1/high_res_d/1002603967-Davidson.pdf)
- Supplement: WiR has Roman-numeral files for Dichterliebe songs (analyst Mark Gotham) and for every Kinderszenen piece (converted from DCML, "Analyst: DCML members and contributors. Licence CC-BY-NC-SA") — [WiR "Ich grolle nicht" analysis.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schumann%2C_Robert/Dichterliebe%2C_Op.48/07_Ich_grolle_nicht/analysis.txt); [WiR Kinderszenen No. 1 analysis.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Schumann%2C_Robert/Kinderszenen%2C_Op.15/1/analysis.txt)

**Mendelssohn candidates (3)**

- Song Without Words Op. 19 No. 1 in E major: a 2021 Rutgers PhD dissertation analyses it in section 2.1 and prints a complete annotated score in Appendix A (pp. 134–138) with a Roman numeral or figure under every harmony, key-change labels, boxed cadence labels (HC, PAC) and phrase/form brackets (Period, antecedent, consequent, Hybrid) — [Kotler, "Mendelssohn's small ternary forms in Songs without words, Op. 19: a quantitative analysis"](https://rucore.libraries.rutgers.edu/rutgers-lib/65438/)

### Inferences
- The pieces that theory teachers actually assign (they appear in Burkhart's anthology, exam-board set works and teoria.com) are the short ones: Preludes 4, 6 and 20, Kinderszenen No. 1, Song Without Words Op. 19 No. 1, "Ich grolle nicht". These are also where the thorough free analyses are, so they are the natural test pieces.
- The "most famous tune" pieces (Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2, Träumerei, Spring Song, Venetian Boat Song) attract listener-oriented or Schenkerian writing rather than chord-by-chord teaching notes. For those, expert chord-label files (DCML, WiR) are the practical substitute where they exist (Träumerei has them; the nocturne and the Mendelssohn pieces do not).
- British exam-board documents (AQA, Pearson Edexcel) use British chord notation (Ib, Vc7, IIb, "interrupted cadence", "imperfect cadence"), so a comparison against the tool's Roman numerals needs a small translation (b = first inversion, c = second inversion).

## For each analysis: URL, author and credentials, date, depth, coverage, annotated scores, and verbatim quotes

### Takeaway
The four most useful analyses for checking the tool are (1) teoria.com on the Chopin preludes (Roman numerals drawn on the score), (2) Kotler's annotated score of Mendelssohn Op. 19 No. 1, (3) the Pearson Edexcel and Sutcliffe analyses of Kinderszenen No. 1, and (4) the AQA guide on Chopin's Nocturne Op. 72 No. 1; each was opened and quoted below. The others are either thinner, by non-specialists, or only partly accessible, and are labelled as such.

### Cited Findings

**A1. teoria.com, Chopin preludes (José Rodríguez Alvira)** — verified by reading every page listed and viewing the score images.

- No. 20 quote: "In Chopin's version, the diminished seventh chord in the 3rd beat of measure 5 is written as a C minor's vii degree but it resolves to a G minor chord." — [Prelude No. 20, measures 5 to 8](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2018/chopin-preludes/20/02.php)
- No. 20 quote: "In measure 8, Chopin uses again the Db major chord, but this time as a Neapolitan sixth chord in root position (it was used in measure 2 as IV degree of Ab major)" — [Prelude No. 20, measures 5 to 8](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2018/chopin-preludes/20/02.php)

### Inferences
- The TPC files declare the very old "MusicXML 0.7b" document type (Finale 2004 export). Twelve of the thirteen I fetched parse as XML, but the tool's loader should be tried on one before committing to this source; the Mendelssohn Op. 19 No. 1 file will probably need re-saving as UTF-8 first (not tested).
- Bar numbering will need reconciling for Mendelssohn Op. 19 No. 1: Kotler's annotated score shows a repeat with first and second endings at bar 15, whereas the TPC file has 62 measures and no repeat or ending marks, so one of the two layouts writes the repeat out.
- When testing with DCML-xml files, the embedded `<harmony>` elements (the expert labels) should be stripped first, both so they do not print on the engraved score and so the tool cannot read the answer. The TPC and Lieder files have no such labels.
- Because the DCML and ASAP material is non-commercial (CC BY-NC-SA), it is fine for internal checking but should not be bundled into a product; OpenScore Lieder (CC0) and the Chopin Institute scores (CC BY) are the safe ones to redistribute. TPC's status is unclear until its author adds a licence.
- GitHub's code search does not index compressed `.mxl` files, so my searches only surfaced uncompressed `.musicxml`/`.xml` files; more `.mxl` copies of these pieces may exist that I could not find this way.

### Gaps
- No good MusicXML of Chopin's Nocturne in E minor Op. 72 No. 1 was found; the one file is a two-track MIDI conversion. A proper engraving probably exists on musescore.com, which needs a login (last resort, not attempted).
- No MusicXML was found for Mendelssohn Op. 19 Nos. 2, 3 or 4 (the other pieces with Kotler's annotated scores), for "Auf Flügeln des Gesanges", or for the original piano text of Schumann's "Wild Rider".
- I did not open the files in notation software, so pitch-level accuracy against a printed edition is unchecked; "verified" means the download succeeded and the structure (parts, staves, key, metre, measure and note counts, title) is plausible.
- The licence of the difficulty-prediction-CIPI example file and of the MuseTrainer files (beyond the "public domain" claim in the README) could not be confirmed.
- The `nereasastre/Music_Corpus_Annotator` repository that hosts the CFE-xml conversions has no licence of its own; the underlying Chopin Institute data is CC BY 4.0, and only some preludes were converted (Nos. 1–6, 8, 10–12, 14–20, 23 across the two editions; No. 7 is not among them).

## Which candidates are the strongest overall, ranked, and why?

### Takeaway
Recommended set: Chopin Prelude No. 20 and Prelude No. 4 (with No. 6 as the alternate), Schumann Kinderszenen No. 1, and Mendelssohn Song Without Words Op. 19 No. 1; each pairs a verified, bar-referenced analysis (most with labels printed on the score) with at least one verified MusicXML file. If the owner prefers the best-known titles, Träumerei and "Ich grolle nicht" are workable for Schumann using expert chord-label files plus lighter prose, but Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2 and the Mendelssohn Spring Song / Venetian Boat Song still lack a thorough free analysis.

### Cited Findings

**Chopin, ranked**

1. Prelude Op. 28 No. 20 in C minor — strongest. Roman numerals under every chord on teoria.com, a second treatment in the Daniel thesis, a human chord-label file in WiR, and three independent score files that agree on 13 measures. Short (13 bars) but dense: modulation, half cadence, Neapolitan, augmented sixth, chromatic bass — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2018/chopin-preludes/20/index.php); [WiR score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Chopin%2C_Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric/Preludes%2C_Op.28/20/score.mxl)
2. Prelude Op. 28 No. 4 in E minor — the deepest teoria article (5 pages, peer reviewed by MERLOT) plus Daniel's reading; three score files agree (26 measure elements). The hardest test of the four: chromatic voice leading, non-chord tones, deceptive cadence, German sixth — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/chopin-prelude-04/index.php); [MuseTrainer score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Prlude_No._4_in_E_Minor_Op._28_-_Frdric_Chopin.mxl)
3. Prelude Op. 28 No. 6 in B minor — the whole piece is annotated on two teoria images (chord symbols and Roman numerals), and Daniel adds pedal tones and key scheme; two score files agree (26 measures) — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2019/chopin-preludes/06/index.php); [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Chopin%2C%20Fryderyk%20%281810%20-%201849%29/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Op28/PreludeOp28No6.xml)
4. Nocturne in E minor Op. 72 No. 1 — the best prose analysis of any Chopin candidate (exam board, bar by bar), but the only score file is a poor MIDI conversion — [AQA guide](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/music/AQA-7272-TG-AOS1-CBG.PDF); [score file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArturoBarrios/MusicClassification/master/MusicXML_Files/AmbroseTabsDatabase/doneClassical/NocturneNo19inEminor%288%29.musicxml)
5. Prelude Op. 28 No. 15 "Raindrop" — the most popular of the preludes and a longer test (89 bars) of form, key areas and pedal points; the analyses cover form, phrases and keys rather than every chord — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2019/chopin-preludes/15/index.php); [CFE-xml score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nereasastre/Music_Corpus_Annotator/main/public/craig_files/humdrum-chopin-first-editions-main/kern/028-1-BH-015.musicxml)
6. Prelude Op. 28 No. 7 in A major — 16 bars; analyses are proportionately short (teoria, Daniel); good as a quick sanity check on sevenths, ninths and appoggiaturas — [teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2019/chopin-preludes/07/index.php); [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Chopin%2C%20Fryderyk%20%281810%20-%201849%29/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Op28/Pr%C3%A9ludeOp28No7.xml)
7. Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2 — the score is fine but the free analysis is a form map with teaching tips, not harmony — [Paperless Piano Teacher](https://paperlesspianoteacher.com/blog/chopin-nocturne-op-9-no-2-analysis); [MuseTrainer score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Chopin_-_Nocturne_Op_9_No_2_E_Flat_Major.mxl)
8. Mazurkas (e.g. Op. 7 No. 1, Op. 17 No. 4, Op. 68 No. 4) — expert chord labels and scores exist, but no whole-piece teacher-style analysis was found — [DCML-xml Op. 7 No. 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/scores/chopin_mazurkas/BI61-1op07-1.musicxml)

**Schumann, ranked**

1. Kinderszenen No. 1 "Von fremden Ländern und Menschen" — two independent analyses that agree on the essentials (official Pearson Edexcel notes; Sutcliffe's annotated outline with non-chord-tone labels), DCML expert labels as a third check, and two score files that agree on 22 measures — [Pearson notes](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Music/2013/Teaching%20and%20learning%20materials/UNIT_3_-_23__Schumann_-_Kinderscenen_Op__15_-_Nos__1_3_and_11_June_2017.pdf); [Sutcliffe outline](https://www.harmony.org.uk/book/schumann_analysis/schumann_outline.pdf); [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Schumann%2C%20Robert%20%281810%20-%201856%29/xml/Childhood%20scenes%20Op15/Op15No1.xml)

Kinderszenen No. 11 "Fürchtenmachen" and No. 3 "Hasche-Mann" — same Pearson notes; richer chromatic content than No. 1 (Neapolitan chords, interrupted cadences, sequences, rondo form), though less famous — [Pearson notes](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Music/2013/Teaching%20and%20learning%20materials/UNIT_3_-_23__Schumann_-_Kinderscenen_Op__15_-_Nos__1_3_and_11_June_2017.pdf); [DCML-xml No. 11](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/scores/schumann_kinderszenen/n11.musicxml)

"Ich grolle nicht" — CC0 voice-and-piano score, a human Roman-numeral file in WiR, and Woltjer's bar-by-bar chord reading (amateur); a good test of a seventh-chord sequence over a descending bass — [Lieder score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/Lieder/main/scores/Schumann%2C_Robert/Dichterliebe%2C_Op.48/07_Ich_grolle_nicht/lc4978382.mxl); [Woltjer](https://geertwoltjer.nl/Songs/DichterliebeAnalysis/Dichterliebe7.html)

5. "Im wunderschönen Monat Mai" — CC0 score and WiR labels; prose analyses are either short (Woltjer, blog) or scholarly-interpretive (Intégral); its tonal ambiguity makes it a poor first test of key detection — [Lieder score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/Lieder/main/scores/Schumann%2C_Robert/Dichterliebe%2C_Op.48/01_Im_wundersch%C3%B6nen_Monat_Mai/lc4976777.mxl); [Intégral article](https://theory.esm.rochester.edu/integral/36-2023/keep/)

**Mendelssohn, ranked**

1. Song Without Words Op. 19 No. 1 in E major — the single best analysis-to-tool match in this whole survey: a complete score with Roman numerals, key changes, cadence labels and phrase functions by a PhD theorist; the score file exists but needs an encoding fix and bar-number mapping — [Kotler dissertation](https://rucore.libraries.rutgers.edu/rutgers-lib/65438/); [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Mendelssohn%2C%20Felix%20%281809%20-%201847%29/xml/Lieder%20ohne%20W%C3%B6rte/Op19No1.xml)
2. Venetian Gondola Song Op. 30 No. 6 — two matching score files (56 measures, 653 notes each); the only substantial analysis is Schenker's essay in a paid book — [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Mendelssohn%2C%20Felix%20%281809%20-%201847%29/xml/Lieder%20ohne%20W%C3%B6rte/Op30No6.xml); [Oxford Academic chapter](https://academic.oup.com/book/49165/chapter/422055905)
3. Spring Song Op. 62 No. 6 — clean 90-measure score; analysis limited to a form outline with bar ranges — [TPC score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Mendelssohn%2C%20Felix%20%281809%20-%201847%29/xml/Lieder%20ohne%20W%C3%B6rte/Op62No6.xml); [Liu paper](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/126014474.pdf)

### Inferences
- Prelude No. 20 and Prelude No. 4 complement each other as the two Chopin pieces: No. 20 is block chords where every label can be compared one to one; No. 4 tests whether the tool can see a simple progression through heavy chromatic voice leading and non-chord tones. If the owner would rather avoid an extreme case, No. 6 is the gentler second choice and its analysis is fully drawn on the score.
- Kinderszenen No. 1 and Mendelssohn Op. 19 No. 1 are both under 65 bars, mostly diatonic with a few tonicizations, and have unambiguous cadences, so disagreements with the tool will be easy to adjudicate.
- All four recommended pieces also appear in the standard classroom anthology (Burkhart) or as exam-board set works, which supports the claim that they are analysed "the way a teacher or textbook might do".
- Nocturne Op. 72 No. 1 would move up to second place among the Chopin pieces if a proper score were obtained, since the AQA analysis is the closest thing to a teacher's written walkthrough.

### Gaps
- The ranking weighs analysis quality and score availability as I could verify them; it does not reflect any trial run of the tool on these files.
- No thorough free analysis was found to make a second or third Mendelssohn candidate "solid"; candidates 2 and 3 are solid on the score side only. Kotler's annotated Op. 19 Nos. 2–4 would be solid on the analysis side if scores were found.
- Whether the owner counts Kinderszenen No. 1 or Op. 19 No. 1 as "popular" enough compared with Träumerei or the Spring Song is a judgement call left to the owner.
