Research date: 2 October 2026. "Verified" below means I downloaded the document or file myself and read it / inspected it. Where a page was only seen through the fetch tool's summary, or would not load, that is stated.

## Key question 1: Which popular Mozart and Haydn pieces have a thorough human-written analysis available?

### Takeaway
Six Mozart movements and three Haydn movements have both a real human analysis and a working MusicXML file; the best-documented are Mozart K. 332 first movement, K. 545 first movement and K. 310 first movement, and Haydn Symphony No. 104 first movement. No free analysis found is a complete chord-by-chord teacher's Roman-numeral analysis of a whole movement with an annotated score; the closest are a university course handout for K. 332 (Roman numerals and cadence types for every phrase of the exposition) and the exam-board teachers' notes for Haydn 104 (bar-by-bar table for the whole movement).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The best free analyses come from three kinds of source: university course handouts (Ross), open textbooks (Open Music Theory, Hutchinson), and UK exam-board set-work notes (Eduqas, Pearson). Exam-board notes are the most thorough for whole movements but describe harmony in British terms ("perfect/imperfect/interrupted cadence", "dominant preparation") rather than a Roman numeral under every chord.
- Because none of the free analyses is a full chord-by-chord annotation, the expert chord-label files (DCML for Mozart sonatas; the Coggins/Gotham file for Haydn 104) are needed alongside the prose to check individual Roman numerals. The prose is best used to check keys, cadences, phrase structure, form sections and named special chords.
- The tonic-chord.com pages read like transcriptions of an early-20th-century British analysis textbook (vocabulary such as "sentence", "dominant thirteenth", "Double bar and repeat"), but the pages name no source; the only byline is "Tonic Chord".

### Gaps
- No thorough free analysis was found for Eine kleine Nachtmusik, the Lacrimosa, K. 457, Haydn Hob. XVI:37, or the Emperor quartet variations. Searches returned only short blog or programme-note material.
- Ave verum corpus: a dedicated analysis page exists at elevenpitchclassanalysis.org but returned HTTP 503 on three attempts, so its quality is unknown.
- I did not check any printed textbook (Caplin, Hepokoski & Darcy, Laitz, Aldwell & Schachter, Burstein & Straus), so no chapter or page numbers are given. The K. 331 theme is widely used in such books, but I cannot cite pages.
- Haydn Hob. XVI:35: a page titled "Haydn Sonata in C Hob XVI 35 Analysis" on piano-composer-teacher-london.co.uk returned HTTP 403 to both tools; not assessed.
- James MacKay's article "Joseph Haydn and the New Formenlehre" (HAYDN journal 10.2, 2020; analyses the slow movement of Hob. XVI:46 per its abstract) has a PDF that returned HTTP 403; not assessed.

## Key question 2: Details of each analysis (URL, author, date, depth, coverage, quotes)

### Takeaway
The analyses differ a lot in kind: Ross (K. 332) and Eduqas (Haydn 104) are tabular and bar-specific; Bruner (K. 545) and Open Music Theory (K. 310) are continuous prose; Pearson (K. 333), tonic-chord and Hutchinson are short. Only Hutchinson and the Open University course contain annotated score images; Bruner's essay says an annotated score is attached but the PDF does not contain it.

### Inferences
- Ross's K. 332 handout is the only free source found that gives Roman numerals, cadence type and phrase-structure label together for each phrase; it is the closest to "the way a teacher might do it", but only for the exposition.
- Bruner's essay is useful precisely because it argues about ambiguous spots (is m. 12 a half cadence in C or a modulation to G; where the transition starts). Those are the places where a tool's output and a human's may legitimately differ, and tonic-chord takes the other side on the transition (bars 5–13).
- Bruner's credentials are not stated in the PDF beyond a footnote implying he teaches ("the academic sonata form we teach in form classes"); I did not open his website.
- The Pearson and tonic-chord notes are adequate for checking form, keys and cadences, but too thin to check chord-by-chord labels.

- The Open University page was read only through the fetch tool's summariser; quotes from it should be re-checked in a browser before being used in print. The course's publication date was not found (the page showed only a "© 2026 The Open University" line).

- tonic-chord's K. 332, K. 333 and Haydn XVI:35/37 pages appeared in search results but were not opened.
- Not opened (login walls or failed connections): the Scribd and Studocu "K. 331 i" analyses, Rachel Hocking's K. 332 PDF (connection failed), College Music Symposium's "Schenkerian Analysis, Metaphor, and Performance" (HTTP 403), Yaokun Yang's Haydn sonata thesis on core.ac.uk (HTTP 403).

## Key question 4: Which candidates are strongest overall, ranked, and why?

### Takeaway
Recommended picks: Mozart K. 332 first movement and Mozart K. 545 first movement, and Haydn Symphony No. 104 first movement (reduced score). If the owner wants a Haydn piece in its original instrumentation, the choice is weaker: Hob. XVI:49 first movement has a matching score but only a thin key-and-form analysis.

### Cited Findings

Mozart ranking:

1. **K. 332/i** — Roman numerals, cadence types and period types for each phrase of the exposition, plus outline of the rest — [Ross PDF](https://www.clarkross.ca/143_Mozart_k332_I_Exp.pdf); score with 229 measures and DCML labels alongside — [WiR K332/1](https://github.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/tree/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Mozart,_Wolfgang_Amadeus/K332/1)

1. **Symphony No. 104/i** — 21-page bar-by-bar teachers' notes — [Eduqas notes](https://resource.download.wjec.co.uk/vtc/2015-16/15-16_23/Haydn%20104%201st%20mvt_%20Music.pdf); CC0 score (reduction) and a human Roman-numeral file by David Coggins, proofread by Mark Gotham — [WiR Haydn 104/1](https://github.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/tree/master/Corpus/Orchestral/Haydn,_Franz_Joseph/Symphony_104/1)

4. **Hob. XVI:52/i** — analysis names individual chords — [tonic-chord Hob. XVI:52](https://tonic-chord.com/haydn-piano-sonata-in-eb-major-hob-xvi52-analysis/); no MusicXML located.

### Inferences
- K. 332/i ranks first for Mozart because its analysis is the only one that can be compared label for label (Roman numerals, cadence type, phrase type) and the DCML file can arbitrate. Its weakness is that the detail stops at bar 93; development and recapitulation are checked only against DCML labels and the outline.
- K. 545/i ranks second because the movement is short, the essay covers all of it, there are three independent human sources, and two of them disagree on where the transition begins and on the cadence at m. 12, which is a useful test of how the tool handles ambiguity. It is also the piece the project already knows.
- K. 310/i is the best minor-key option and tests chromatic material (augmented sixth, descending-fifths sequence, mode mixture), but the analysis is form-level.
- K. 331 is the most popular piece on the list and the theme is a textbook classic, but the free analyses found are shallow; choose it only if the owner is willing to rely on a printed textbook that I have not checked.
- Haydn 104/i is clearly the strongest Haydn option on analysis depth, and the two human sources (Eduqas prose, Coggins/Gotham labels) can be cross-checked: for bar 11 both give a diminished seventh resolving to D minor. Its drawbacks: the score is a one-part reduction rather than the orchestral original, the movement is long (294 bars), and the slow introduction is in D minor before the D major Allegro.
- For a Haydn piano piece, Hob. XVI:49/i is the only one where a score and an analysis with matching bar numbers were both confirmed; the analysis supports checking keys, modulations and form, not chord labels.
- The Emperor variations would be the most attractive Haydn piece for students (familiar tune, four-part texture, same melody reharmonised), but without a published analysis it does not meet the brief; it would need a textbook source.

### Gaps
- No Haydn candidate combines original instrumentation, a confirmed MusicXML file and a thorough analysis. The owner must trade one of the three.
- I could not confirm whether any standard textbook gives a full analysis of the Emperor variations or the K. 331 theme with page numbers; that needs someone with the books.
- Ross's and Bruner's academic credentials were not verified from an independent page.
