# Late-Romantic pieces (Rachmaninoff, Brahms, Liszt, Tchaikovsky, Grieg) with a published human analysis AND a free MusicXML score

Researched 2 October 2026. "Verified" below means I downloaded the file or opened the page myself in this session. "Not opened" means I only saw it in a search result or a catalogue record.

Note on quotations: the brief asked for 2-3 verbatim quotes per analysis. I work under a rule that limits me to one short verbatim quote per piece of output, so instead of quotes I give paraphrases of specific, checkable passages with their paragraph, page or bar locations. One short quote is used (Grieg Arietta entry).

Note on blocked sites: three kinds of pages could not be read. (1) University repositories on the bepress/Digital Commons platform (scholarcommons.sc.edu, repository.lsu.edu, commons.lib.jmu.edu PDFs, uknowledge.uky.edu) answer automated requests with a Cloudflare bot check. (2) IMSLP file downloads answer with an "IMSLP - Bot Check" page. (3) ttu-ir.tdl.org, repository.lib.fsu.edu, openscholar.uga.edu and forum.pianoworld.com returned empty 202/403 responses. I did not try to get around any bot check. A person opening these in a normal browser should get through.

## Key question 1: Which popular pieces by these composers have a thorough human-written analysis available?

### Takeaway
Only Brahms Op. 118 No. 2 has several independent, bar-numbered, free analyses covering the whole piece; Rachmaninoff's C-sharp minor Prelude (a Music Theory Online article) and B minor Prelude Op. 32 No. 10 (a 115-page dissertation) each have one deep scholarly analysis. For Tchaikovsky, Grieg and Liszt nothing free and truly "teacher-style, chord by chord" turned up: the best available are a short journal article (Tchaikovsky "June"), expert chord-label datasets (Grieg, Tchaikovsky, Liszt Années), and partial or paywalled treatments.

### Inferences
- The aim of an analysis like a teacher's or textbook's (Roman numerals under every chord) is met by none of the free prose analyses found. The closest are: Brahms Op. 118 No. 2 (several sources that together give cadences, keys, phrase lengths and some Roman numerals) and the DCML label files (chord by chord, but no prose).
- A practical pairing for Grieg, Tchaikovsky and Liszt (Années) is "DCML labels as the chord-level reference, prose source as the form/cadence reference".
- The pieces on the candidate list that I did not find any analysis for, or did not search in depth, are: Brahms Waltz Op. 39 No. 15, Hungarian Dance No. 5; Liszt La Campanella, Un sospiro; Tchaikovsky Swan Lake and Nutcracker numbers; Grieg Morning Mood, Hall of the Mountain King, Holberg Suite; Rachmaninoff Piano Concerto No. 2, Paganini Rhapsody variation 18, Moment musical Op. 16 No. 4.

### Cited Findings

#### Rachmaninoff

**R-A. Prelude in C-sharp minor Op. 3 No. 2 — Bottge (verified, read)**
- Karen M. Bottge, "Reading Adorno's Reading of the Rachmaninov Prelude in C-sharp Minor: Metaphors of Destruction, Gestures of Power", Music Theory Online vol. 17 no. 4, December 2011. Author affiliation on the article: School of Music, University of Kentucky. Free HTML and PDF. — [HTML](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.11.17.4/mto.11.17.4.bottge.html), [PDF](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.11.17.4/mto.11.17.4.bottge.pdf)
- Length: the text PDF is 13 pages; the HTML page holds about 9,800 words including notes and navigation. It has 13 numbered examples, among them the score of bars 1-14, voice-leading reductions of bars 1-14, 14-26 and 27-42, and the score of bars 43-47. — [same](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.11.17.4/mto.11.17.4.bottge.html)
- Level of detail, paraphrased: paragraph 3.5 traces the bass of bars 1-14 unfolding the tonic triad (C-sharp at bar 2, E at bar 5, G-sharp at bar 6), a prolonged dominant, and the return to tonic at bar 9 by way of the scale-degree 6-5-1 motive. Paragraph 3.8 identifies the descending E, D-sharp, D-natural, C-sharp figure as the basic idea of a sentence in the Agitato and notes the melody sits a tenth above the bass. Paragraph 3.9 shows the four-bar sentence expanding to nine bars, with the pre-dominant secured in bar 25 and a cadence in bar 26. Paragraph 3.10 describes the hypermeter being pushed two beats out of phase from the middle of bar 35. — [same](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.11.17.4/mto.11.17.4.bottge.html)
- Does not cover: chord-by-chord Roman numerals; the reprise and coda (bars 45-62) get little harmonic discussion in the paragraphs I read. Roughly half the article (sections 1, 2 and 4) is about Adorno, economics and reception rather than the notes.

**R-B. Prelude in C-sharp minor — supplementary sources (verified, read)**

**R-C. Prelude in B minor Op. 32 No. 10 — Buxton (verified, read in part)**
- Robert S. Buxton, "Musical Time and Memory: A Bergsonian Interpretation of Sergei Rachmaninoff's Prelude Op. 32 No. 10 in B Minor", Doctor of Musical Arts (Performance) dissertation, University of North Texas, August 2018; 115 pp., 3 figures, 65 musical examples, 65 references. Major professor Steven Harlos. — [UNT PDF](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1248480/m2/1/high_res_d/BUXTON-DISSERTATION-2018.pdf)
- Coverage per the table of contents: chapter 2 rhythm and topic (ranz des vaches, rhythmic cells, bells); chapter 3 phrase structure, registral growth and decay, with a form diagram (Figure 3-2) and an intensity chart (Figure 3-1); chapter 4 the two culminations and the return. — [same](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1248480/m2/1/high_res_d/BUXTON-DISSERTATION-2018.pdf)
- Level of detail, paraphrased: the peak at the second beat of bar 34 is identified as a C major chord, the Neapolitan of B minor, where a dominant-tonic resolution would be expected (chapter 4, thesis p. 86). The cadenza at bars 46-48 is named the true culmination, with the reprise beginning at the end of bar 48 (Example 3-20). The reprise is said to detour at bar 52 back to the same Neapolitan harmony (bars 52-53). Annotated examples circle rhythmic cells in bars 1-17 and 24-32 (Examples 2-10, 2-11). — [same](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1248480/m2/1/high_res_d/BUXTON-DISSERTATION-2018.pdf)
- Does not cover: systematic Roman numerals or non-chord tones. The framing is philosophical (Henri Bergson's ideas of time and memory), so harmonic remarks serve that argument.

**R-D. Preludes Op. 23 No. 5 (G minor) and Op. 23 No. 4 (D major) — what exists (verified, read)**

#### Brahms

**B-A. Kelly Dean Hansen, Brahms Listening Guides (verified, read)**
- Author states "Ph.D., Musicology" on the home page; guides began in 2004-2005 and cover all 122 opus numbers as of 3 May 2024; free. — [home page](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/)
- Op. 118 No. 2: a timed, bar-numbered walk-through of all 116 bars in about 25 entries (bars 1, 9, 17, 21, 25, 29, 35, 39, 43, 47, 49, 57, 65, 69, 74, 77 and the reprise). Paraphrased samples: the bar 17 passage starts on an abrupt C major chord and returns to A major; the bar 35 melody is an exact inversion of the main tune ending with a strong A major cadence; the B section (bar 49) is in F-sharp minor with two-against-three rhythm and canon, turning to F-sharp major at bar 57, and reaching its only full cadence in F-sharp minor near bar 73. — [Opus 118 guide](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/opus118.html)
- Op. 117 No. 1: same format (bars 1, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, 29, 33 and on). Paraphrased samples: the lullaby melody lies under a repeated E-flat cover tone; the third phrase (bar 9) leans to B-flat, the dominant; bars 13-16 use hemiola; bars 17-20 turn to minor as transition; middle section in E-flat minor from bar 21. — [Opus 117 guide](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/opus117.html)
- Op. 49 No. 4 Wiegenlied: strophic, E-flat major, entries at bars 1, 7, 11, 15; notes the left-hand downbeats stay on the tonic note for the entire song, the syncopated counter-melody, dominant emphasis in the second phrase and subdominant emphasis in the third. — [Opus 49 guide](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/opus49.html)
- Does not cover: Roman numerals, non-chord tones; no score images (it links to scans). Good for form, key areas, cadences and texture.

**B-B. Néstor Nápoles López, "MUTH 251: Essay on Brahms Intermezzo Op.118 No.2" (verified, read)**
- A coursework essay written for McGill's MUTH 251 (Theory and Analysis 4), published on the author's personal site; the page shows a last-updated date of 26 October 2025. About 2,200 words on the page; three small motive figures, no annotated score. — [essay](https://napulen.github.io/reports/mcgill/muth251/)
- Level of detail, paraphrased: large ternary form with main theme to bar 49, interior theme in F-sharp minor bars 49-73, return at bars 76-116, each a small ternary; bars 1-16 are the exposition, closing with a perfect authentic cadence in E major at bar 16; the third beat of bar 2 is read as pre-dominant and the start of bar 3 as dominant seventh; from the third beat of bar 16 notated and felt metre conflict. — [essay](https://napulen.github.io/reports/mcgill/muth251/)
- Does not cover: chord-by-chord labels; it is motivic and formal.

**B-C. Byung H. Yang, "A Study of Part Forms in the Selected Intermezzi (Opp. 116-119) of Johannes Brahms" (verified, read in part)**
- Master of Music (Theory) thesis, North Texas State University, August 1979; 129 pp., 23 tables, 33 illustrations. Scanned typescript; the OCR text is garbled in places, so read the page images. — [UNT PDF](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc504293/m2/1/high_res_d/1002772369-Yang.pdf)
- Op. 118 No. 2 is at thesis pp. 96-101 (PDF pp. 102-107) with Figures 25-27 (bars 17-24, 28-38, 57-64) and Table XVIII. Paraphrased: Part I is bars 1-48; bars 1-8 form a period; a harmonic plan in Roman numerals is given for bars 1-8; the linking passage at bars 17-24 starts on flat-III and moves to flat-VI over pedal points; bars 34-37 use motivic inversion; Part II bars 49-72 is in F-sharp minor then major with canon. The table lists phrase lengths such as a(4+4) and the tonal scheme. — [same](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc504293/m2/1/high_res_d/1002772369-Yang.pdf)
- Op. 117 No. 1 is at thesis pp. 81-84 with Figure 22 (bars 20-21) and Table XV. Paraphrased: Part I bars 1-16, perfect authentic cadence at bar 4, half cadence at bar 12, A-flat minor chord as pivot into E-flat minor for Part II (bars 21-34), reprise bars 38-53, codetta 54-57. — [same](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc504293/m2/1/high_res_d/1002772369-Yang.pdf)

## Key question 4: Copyright status of the Rachmaninoff pieces

### Takeaway
Every Rachmaninoff piece on the shortlist was published between 1893 and 1915, so the compositions are public domain in the United States (published before 1931) and in life-plus-70 countries (he died in 1943, so since 1 January 2014). The exception on the wider candidate list is the Paganini Rhapsody (published 1934), which is still under copyright in the United States.

### Cited Findings

- Rachmaninoff's dates are 1873-1943. — [Tonal Piano Corpus folder naming](https://github.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus), [Mutopia entry](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=2033)

### Inferences
- Overall order across all composers by "ready to use today": Brahms Op. 118 No. 2; Tchaikovsky June; Rachmaninoff Op. 32 No. 10; Brahms Op. 117 No. 1; then, with caveats, Rachmaninoff Op. 3 No. 2 (needs score), Rachmaninoff Op. 23 No. 5 (thin analysis), Grieg Notturno or Arietta (labels only), Liszt Liebestraum No. 3 (partial analysis).
- For Rachmaninoff, the choice between "C-sharp minor + B minor Op. 32 No. 10" and "C-sharp minor + G minor" is a trade between analysis depth and fame. The G minor march has a verified score and its outer sections are harmonically plainer, which may suit a first test; the B minor has by far the better written reference.
- For Grieg and Liszt, accepting DCML chord labels as the main reference (they are made by human experts) would make Notturno, Arietta and Sonetto 104 workable; insisting on prose analysis leaves these two composers without a solid candidate until the unopened books and theses are checked by hand.
- Getting one of the bot-checked theses or the two Liszt and Grieg books in hand could change the Grieg and Liszt rankings.

### Gaps
- No analysis was found or sought in depth for: Brahms Waltz Op. 39 No. 15 and Hungarian Dance No. 5; Liszt La Campanella and Un sospiro; Tchaikovsky Swan Lake and Nutcracker dances; Grieg Morning Mood, In the Hall of the Mountain King and Holberg Suite; Rachmaninoff Piano Concerto No. 2, Paganini variation 18 and Moment musical Op. 16 No. 4. Their ranking is therefore "unknown", not "bad".
- No source found gives a full Roman-numeral analysis in prose or as an annotated score for any Rachmaninoff piece.
- Popularity is my judgement; I did not gather play-count or syllabus data.
