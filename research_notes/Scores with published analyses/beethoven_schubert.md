# Beethoven and Schubert pieces with a published human analysis and a free MusicXML score

Research date: 2 October 2026. Everything marked "verified" was opened or downloaded in this session. Items I could not open are listed under Gaps for each question.

Short version of the result: score files are easy (every candidate below has a verified, freely downloadable MusicXML file). Free online teacher-style analyses that give chord-by-chord harmony for a whole movement were NOT found for any candidate. What exists online is (a) form-and-key outlines with bar numbers, (b) textbook figures that annotate one phrase, and (c) expert chord-label datasets. The realistic check for the tool is to combine (a)+(b) with (c), piece by piece.

## Which popular Beethoven and Schubert pieces have a thorough human-written analysis available?

### Takeaway
No candidate has a single free analysis that meets the full "thorough" bar (whole piece, bar numbers, Roman numerals, cadences, phrases, non-chord tones). The best-covered pieces are Beethoven Op. 2 No. 1 first movement, Pathétique second and third movements, Moonlight first movement, and Schubert "Der Lindenbaum" and "Gute Nacht", each covered by a form/key outline with bar numbers plus at least one full expert chord-label file.

### Inferences
- The tonic-chord.com texts read like an early-1900s British form primer ("half-close", "connecting episode", "full close"). They are probably republished from an old public-domain book rather than written in 2018. I did not confirm the source.
- For checking the tool's Roman numerals, the When in Rome label files are the only whole-movement human reference found. For checking form, key areas and cadences "the way a teacher would", the outlines and textbook figures are usable.
- The two Boston University worksheets could serve as ready-made test questions for the tool (e.g. "what suspension is in m. 21 of Gute Nacht?") even though they give no answers.

### Gaps
- Tovey's Companion and Caplin's "Classical Form" (which I recall opens with Op. 2 No. 1 bars 1-8 as Example 1.1) could not be opened, so I cannot give verified page numbers. Laitz and Aldwell & Schachter were not checked.
- A Laitz "Complete Musician" chapter 22 PDF (chromatic modulation and the German Lied) appeared in search results at music.arts.uci.edu but now returns "Sorry, this page does not exist"; no Wayback copy. Whether it analyses "Der Lindenbaum" is unverified.
- Two pages were blocked (HTTP 403 / Cloudflare) and remain unverified: a "Für Elise Complete Analysis" at piano-composer-teacher-london.co.uk, and a Journal of Music Theory Pedagogy e-journal PDF at digitalcollections.lipscomb.edu that a search snippet linked to Op. 2 No. 1.
- The 12 images on the musicalibra Moonlight page and the Hutchinson Moonlight and Für Elise figures were not viewed, so I cannot say how they are annotated.
- No teacher-style analysis was searched for in depth for Symphony No. 5, Ode to Joy, the Op. 18 quartets, "Ave Maria", "Die Forelle", "Gretchen am Spinnrade", Impromptu Op. 90 No. 4, Moment musical No. 3 or the "Unfinished" Symphony. Video analyses were not used.
- I did not read the Schubert Winterreise Dataset annotation files; which annotation types it holds beyond the Zenodo summary is unverified.

## For each analysis: URL, author, date, depth, coverage, annotated images, verbatim quotes

### Takeaway
Seven sources are worth using; all were opened and quoted below. None gives chord-by-chord harmony for a whole piece except the When in Rome label files.

### Cited Findings

- tonic-chord.com Beethoven sonata series. Author shown only as "Tonic Chord"; Sonata No. 1 page dated May 17, 2018. About 900 words per sonata, all movements. Covers form, section bar ranges, keys, some cadences and enharmonic modulations. Does not cover chord-by-chord harmony, non-chord tones or voice leading. Score images: the No. 1 and No. 8 pages carry 16-18 images that I did not view. Quotes: "Bars 1-9: 1st subject in F minor (tonic). The 1st subject ends at bar 9 on a half-close. Quotes: "The first episode (the B section) is in the mediant, Eb major, the relative major of C minor." / "A retransition follows the second episode, ending on a half cadence on G major." / "Beethoven changes the intervals of subphrase a in measures 3–4 to fit the dominant harmony. ### Inferences
- Sources disagree on bar numbering for Op. 2 No. 1 first movement: tonic-chord and Music Theory Academy put the recapitulation at bar 103, while the Erçelik blog puts it at m. 101 and the BPS label file numbers the upbeat as m0. Any comparison must first align numbering (upbeat and first/second endings).
- Sources also disagree on where Moonlight's sections fall only in the one rejected page; tonic-chord, musicalibra and Stevenson agree on the recapitulation at bar 42 and the coda at bar 60.

### Gaps
- Author credentials for tonic-chord.com and musicalibra.com are unknown.
- Edition, date and licence of the Hutchinson textbook were not captured from its front matter.
- Wikipedia sections are community-written; I did not trace their footnotes.

### Inferences
- The ASAP file for Op. 2 No. 1 has 153 numbered measures and no repeat signs, against the 152 bars of the printed movement; the upbeat is probably numbered as measure 1, so bar numbers may be offset by one from the label files.
- The Für Elise file's 106 measures with endings will not match the Wikipedia bar numbers (B section at bar 23, cadence at bar 76) without a mapping.
- For non-commercial testing all files are usable; if the tool or its test fixtures are ever distributed commercially, only the CC0 Schubert songs are clearly safe.

### Gaps
- I checked file type, size, parts and measure count only; I did not render the files or proof-read notes against a printed edition.
- MuseTrainer's "public domain" claim is unsupported by any per-file provenance.
- KernScores/humdrum Beethoven sonatas were not located (the GitHub repository name I tried does not exist).
- No MusicXML was sought for the Ode to Joy theme in original scoring, the "Unfinished" Symphony or Symphony No. 5 in full score.

## Which candidates are the strongest overall, ranked, and why?

### Takeaway
Beethoven: (1) Pathétique second movement, (2) Op. 2 No. 1 first movement, (3) Moonlight first movement. Schubert: (1) "Der Lindenbaum", with "Gute Nacht" as runner-up. These have the best pairing of bar-numbered human commentary, a full expert chord-label file and a verified score.

### Cited Findings

Beethoven ranking

1. Pathétique, second movement. Short (73 measures), two verified scores, an outline that pins down the hard spots (enharmonic moves at bars 42 and 48), an annotated textbook figure for bars 1-8 with cadences, and a full DCML label file. — [tonic-chord](https://tonic-chord.com/beethoven-piano-sonata-no-8-in-c-minor-pathetique-analysis/); [Hutchinson](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/PeriodForm.html); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Sonate_No._8_Pathetique_2nd_Movement.mxl)
2. Op. 2 No. 1, first movement. The only candidate with a whole-movement annotated score PDF, plus three separate expert label files and the textbook sentence figure. Weakness: bar-number offsets between sources. — [annotated PDF](https://www.musictheoryacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Beethoven-sonata-in-F-minor-sonata-form-annotated-sheet-music.pdf); [labels](https://github.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/tree/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Beethoven,_Ludwig_van/Op002_No1/1); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Beethoven/Piano_Sonatas/1-1/xml_score.musicxml)
3. Moonlight, first movement. Short (69 measures), three prose sources that agree on the structure, two label files. Weaknesses: the fullest prose source is anonymous, and the only MusicXML has unstated licence and a 4/4 time signature. — [tonic-chord](https://tonic-chord.com/beethoven-piano-sonata-no-14-in-c-minor-analysis/); [labels](https://github.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/tree/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Beethoven,_Ludwig_van/Op027_No2(Moonlight)/1); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Sonate_No._14_Moonlight_1st_Movement.mxl)
4. Pathétique, third movement. Best textbook walk-through of a whole movement (11 figures), full label file, verified score; longer (210 bars). — [Hutchinson](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/RondoForm.html); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Beethoven/Piano_Sonatas/8-3/xml_score.musicxml)
5. Für Elise. Most popular, decent bar-referenced prose, but no expert chord-label file and a score with unstated licence. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCr_Elise); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Fur_Elise.mxl)
6. Tempest, Waldstein, Appassionata first movements. Verified scores and label files, but long and with no teacher-style commentary verified. — [When in Rome](https://github.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/tree/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Beethoven,_Ludwig_van)

Schubert ranking

1. "Der Lindenbaum". CC0 score; a manual Roman-numeral file with form labels and mixture/augmented-sixth chords (the Schubert features the owner wants tested); thesis commentary with score examples; also in the Winterreise dataset. — [labels](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schubert,_Franz/Winterreise,_D.911/05_Der_Lindenbaum/analysis.txt); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schubert,_Franz/Winterreise,_D.911/05_Der_Lindenbaum/score.mxl)
2. "Gute Nacht". CC0 score, manual label file with six key changes, a teacher's bar-specific worksheet, thesis commentary. Repeat signs in the score complicate bar numbering (the label file notes a "music21 repeat issue"). — [labels](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schubert,_Franz/Winterreise,_D.911/01_Gute_Nacht/analysis.txt); [worksheet](https://people.bu.edu/burtond/resources/Teaching/5a8.401Handouts.pdf)
3. "Erlkönig". Most famous and has a clear whole-song key table, but no chord-label file and a 148-bar through-composed texture. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlk%C3%B6nig_(Schubert)); [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/Lieder/main/scores/Schubert,_Franz/_/Der_Erlk%C3%B6nig,_D.328/lc29062370.mxl)
4. "Ständchen". CC0 score, manual label file, teacher's worksheet on mixture; no prose analysis. — [labels](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schubert,_Franz/Schwanengesang,_D.957/04_St%C3%A4ndchen/analysis.txt)
5. Impromptu Op. 90 No. 3. Verified score but only a thin blog analysis and no label file found. — [pianistmusings](https://pianistmusings.com/2019/02/22/schubert-impromptu-g-flat-major-op-90-no-3-analysis/)

### Inferences
- The ranking weights three things equally: a whole-piece human reference for chords, bar-numbered teacher-style prose for form/keys/cadences, and a short clean score. It is my judgement, not a sourced fact.
- If the owner wants one sonata-form piece and one slow piece, pairing Op. 2 No. 1 i with Pathétique ii covers sentence, sonata form, rondo, period, and enharmonic modulation.
- If a book is acceptable, borrowing Tovey's Companion on archive.org would likely upgrade any of the sonata candidates to a true bar-by-bar reference; this is untested.

### Gaps
- No candidate has a verified free source for non-chord tones or voice leading across a whole piece.
- The ranking would change if the blocked Für Elise and Journal of Music Theory Pedagogy pages turn out to be thorough.
