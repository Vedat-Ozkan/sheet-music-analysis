# Eleven of twenty-three slots pair cleanly

Every one of the 23 requested slots can be given a named piece, but only **11 have both a thorough, bar-numbered human analysis and a complete MusicXML file that downloaded and parsed**: all three Bach, both Mozart, both Chopin, Schumann, Brahms and both Debussy. Nine more are usable with one named weakness. For Beethoven, Schubert and Tchaikovsky the free prose is a form-and-key outline, and the chord-by-chord reference is an expert label file with no explanation attached. Haydn's only strong pairing uses a reduced score, Mendelssohn has the best analysis in the whole survey but a file that fails to parse, and the two Ravel analyses and one Rachmaninoff analysis are doctoral studies that do not go chord by chord. **Three slots stay open**: no thorough analysis was found for any Liszt or Grieg piece, and Rachmaninoff's Prelude in C-sharp minor has a peer-reviewed analysis but no confirmed score file. Because the analysis was looked for first, the picks are the pieces theory teachers assign (Well-Tempered Clavier, a chorale, Chopin preludes, exam-board set works), and several of the most famous titles (Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2, Träumerei, Für Elise, Clair de lune, Liebestraum No. 3, the Goldberg Aria) turned out to have no thorough free analysis. Before any run, three things need handling: bar-number maps between analysis and file, licences that are non-commercial or missing, and expert chord labels embedded inside some files. No file was run through the tool or proofread against a printed edition; "confirmed" means downloaded on 2 October 2026 and checked for structure only.

The table gives the recommended piece for each slot in the order requested. "Ready" means a human analysis that walks the piece with bar numbers in the way a teacher would, plus a complete score in the original instrumentation that downloaded and parsed. "Caveat" means both halves exist but one is weaker, and the weakness is named. "Open" means one half is missing. Licence problems do not change the status here, since they matter for redistribution and not for an internal test; they are covered in the last section.

One finding cuts across the table. No free source gives a Roman numeral under every chord for a whole sonata movement by Mozart, Haydn or Beethoven, and none does so for any Rachmaninoff, Liszt, Grieg or Tchaikovsky piece. The full label-on-the-score analyses that exist are for short pieces: the Bach chorale, the Chopin preludes, Schumann's Kinderszenen No. 1 and Mendelssohn's Op. 19 No. 1. For the longer movements the realistic check is a pair of references: prose for form, keys and cadences, and an expert chord-label file for the individual numerals.

## Bach and Mozart are well served; Haydn, Beethoven and Schubert lean on label files

### Bach: one arpeggiated prelude, one chorale, one fugue

The three Bach picks cover three different kinds of writing, and each has a professional analysis and a verified file. For the **Prelude in C major, BWV 846**, Siglind Bruhn's book chapter (a music analyst and concert pianist, formerly a research associate at the University of Michigan; book pages 49 to 52) gives four sections with bar ranges, Roman numerals for the cadences, the sequences, the pedal points and a chord reduction of the whole prelude; it does not give a numeral for every bar ([Bruhn](https://edition-gorz.de/WTC-01.pdf)). Two further human readings fill that in: José Rodríguez Alvira's ten short pages on teoria.com go chord by chord through bars 1 to 23 ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2017/BWV846/index.php)), and Mark Gotham's label file in the When in Rome corpus gives one numeral for each of the 35 bars ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Bach,_Johann_Sebastian/The_Well-Tempered_Clavier_I/01/analysis.txt)). Bruhn and Gotham agree on the opening (I, ii2, V65, I) and on the dominant pedal at bar 24. The score in the same folder has 35 measures and a clear licence ([When in Rome README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/README.md)). A second good copy sits in the ASAP dataset, also 35 measures, under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([ASAP file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Bach/Prelude/bwv_846/xml_score.musicxml)). **Avoid the music21 copy**, which has only 34 measure elements and will not line up with any bar-numbered analysis ([music21 file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cuthbertLab/music21/master/music21/corpus/bach/bwv846.mxl)).

The **chorale "Aus meines Herzens Grunde", BWV 269**, is the one Bach piece whose published analysis has the same shape as the tool's output. Alexander Martin, Assistant Professor of Music Theory at Stetson University, wrote about 700 words in six phrase sections and drew a full annotated score: a Roman numeral under every chord, boxed cadence labels at the fermatas, labelled passing notes, neighbour notes, suspensions and retardations, and bar numbers ([Martin, 17 July 2017](https://alexandermartinblog.wordpress.com/2017/07/17/1-aus-meines-herzens-grunde/)). A second human reading exists as a label file by Andrew Jones, proofread by Dmitri Tymoczko and Hamish Robb ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cuthbertLab/music21/master/music21/corpus/bach/choraleAnalyses/riemenschneider001.rntxt)). The four-part score parsed correctly; its 24 measure elements run to a last bar number of 21, most likely because of the pickup and split bars at the repeat. Its licence is only the general music21 corpus notice, which says works are out of copyright in the United States or licensed, possibly with limits on commercial use ([corpus notice](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cuthbertLab/music21/master/music21/corpus/license.txt)).

For the **Fugue in C minor, BWV 847**, Bruhn lists all eight subject entries by bar and voice, both countersubjects, the six episodes and their motifs, the key plan and a design diagram of all 31 bars (book pages 66 to 72); she does not label chords through the episodes ([Bruhn](https://edition-gorz.de/WTC-02.pdf)). Robert Hutchinson's open textbook prints the whole fugue as annotated figures marking expositions, episodes and countersubjects ([Hutchinson, section 30.8](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/FugueAnalysis.html)). These analyses test form and key areas more than chord numerals. The 31-measure file matches Bruhn's numbering but carries no rights statement. Two alternates of the same music exist: another two-staff copy ([gong repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fullstack-lang/gong/main/app/xsd/tests/musicxml/Fugue_No._2_BWV_847_in_C_Minor.musicxml)) and a version with each voice on its own staff, which may suit voice-leading output better ([sightread repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/marycourtland/sightread/master/examples/Bach_WTC_Fugue_2_in_Cm_BWV_847.xml)).

The best substitutes, if three chord-by-chord pieces are preferred over a fugue, are the Prelude in C minor BWV 847 (Bruhn plus Gotham's labels; 38-measure When in Rome file under CC BY-SA 4.0, [score](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Bach,_Johann_Sebastian/The_Well-Tempered_Clavier_I/02/score.mxl)) and the Invention No. 13 in A minor, whose teoria.com pages print Roman numerals and key areas under the whole score with very little prose ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2020/bach-inventio/13/index.php); 25-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/webern/mx/main/data/foundsuite/Invention_13.xml), no rights statement). The Fugue in C major BWV 846 ([27-measure ASAP file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Bach/Fugue/bwv_846/xml_score.musicxml)) and Invention No. 1 ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/BWV772/index.php); 22-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/webern/mx/main/data/foundsuite/Invention%201.xml)) follow.

The famous Bach titles outside the teaching repertoire did not work out. The Goldberg Aria has a good 32-measure file ([freedots](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlang/freedots/master/scores/bwv988-aria.xml)) but the only analysis found is a 350-word student post with an error a reader pointed out ([DePauw class blog](http://depauwform.blogspot.com/2005/02/aria-from-js-bachs-goldberg-variations.html)). The Cello Suite No. 1 Prelude has no MusicXML, only a MuseScore file ([DCML](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DCMLab/bach_solo/main/MS3/BWV1007_01_Prelude.mscx)), and its free analysis is a short guitarist's post in a transposed key ([Hall](https://johnhallguitar.com/blog/blog/bach-cello-suite-1-bwv-1007-prelude-analysis)). The Prelude BWV 999 has an analysis in C minor ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2024/BWV999/index.php)) but the only file is transposed to D minor ([freedots](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlang/freedots/master/scores/bwv999.xml)). The Air and the Toccata and Fugue in D minor exist only as piano arrangements, and the Minuet in G is credited in its own file to Christian Petzold ([MuseTrainer file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Bach_Minuet_in_G_Major_BWV_Anh._114.mxl)). No thorough free analysis was found for the Air, "Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring" or the Toccata.

### Mozart: K. 332 and K. 545, with K. 310 as the minor-key alternate

For **K. 332, first movement**, a four-page course handout headed "Dr. C. Ross" (course Mu3107, Analysis of Musical Form) is the only free source found that gives Roman numerals, cadence type and phrase type together for each phrase. It does so for the exposition, bars 1 to 93; the development and recapitulation are in outline only, and it has no annotated score ([Ross](https://www.clarkross.ca/143_Mozart_k332_I_Exp.pdf)). The When in Rome file has 229 measures, matching Ross's bar numbers, and the same folder holds an expert chord-label file for the whole movement that covers the bars Ross only outlines ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Mozart,_Wolfgang_Amadeus/K332/1/analysis.txt)). A second copy of the score is in ASAP, also 229 measures ([ASAP file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Mozart/Piano_Sonatas/12-1/xml_score.musicxml)).

For **K. 545, first movement**, Richard Bruner's nine-page essay (dated 8/1/2024) walks through all 73 bars naming chords, cadences and key areas. It says an annotated score is attached, but the PDF contains text only ([Bruner](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e5b68dff2456f2d5ba27693/t/6742d8df45da2d598bcd016a/1732434144131/Richard+Bruner+Mozart+K.+545+Analysis.pdf)). A second form reading disagrees with Bruner on where the transition starts ([tonic-chord.com](https://tonic-chord.com/mozart-piano-sonata-no-16-in-c-major-k-545-analysis/)), and an Open University course analyses the voice leading of bars 1 to 8 with annotated examples ([OpenLearn](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/music/voice-leading-analysis-music-1-the-foreground/content-section-3.3.1/?printable=1)). The disagreements at bars 5 to 13 are useful, since they mark places where the tool may differ from one human without being wrong.

The first alternate is K. 310, first movement, which the Open Music Theory textbook uses as its worked sonata-form example: about 900 words on bars 1 to 133 covering keys, cadences and a few special chords, not chord by chord ([Open Music Theory](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/sonata-form/); 133-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Mozart,_Wolfgang_Amadeus/K310/1/score.mxl)). K. 333, first movement, has four pages of exam-board notes that are thin on chords ([Pearson Edexcel](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Music/2013/Teaching%20and%20learning%20materials/Unit%203%20-%2022.%20Mozart%20Piano%20Sonata%20in%20B%20flat,%20K.%20333%20movement%20I.pdf); 167-element [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Mozart,_Wolfgang_Amadeus/K333/1/score.mxl)). K. 331 is the most popular piece on the list, but its free analyses are shallow: a textbook figure for bars 1 to 8 of the theme ([Hutchinson](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/PeriodForm.html)) and a bar-numbered outline ([tonic-chord.com](https://tonic-chord.com/mozart-piano-sonata-no-11-in-a-major-k-331-analysis/)); files exist for the first movement ([137 elements](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Mozart,_Wolfgang_Amadeus/K331/1/score.mxl)) and the Alla Turca ([137 elements](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Mozart,_Wolfgang_Amadeus/K331/3/score.mxl)). No thorough free analysis was found for Eine kleine Nachtmusik, the Lacrimosa or Symphony No. 40, and no MusicXML was found for those, for Ave verum corpus or for the Fantasia K. 397.

### Haydn: the strongest analysis comes with a reduced score

**Symphony No. 104, first movement**, is clearly the best-documented Haydn piece. The WJEC/Eduqas teachers' notes run to 21 pages with a section-by-section, bar-by-bar table for the whole movement, naming keys, cadences, pedals, sequences and special chords (Neapolitan sixth, diminished seventh, augmented sixth), though not a numeral for every chord ([Eduqas notes](https://resource.download.wjec.co.uk/vtc/2015-16/15-16_23/Haydn%20104%201st%20mvt_%20Music.pdf)). A human Roman-numeral file by David Coggins, proofread by Mark Gotham, sits next to the score, and the two sources agree where they were compared (a diminished seventh resolving to D minor in bar 11) ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Orchestral/Haydn,_Franz_Joseph/Symphony_104/1/analysis.txt)). The caveat is the file: it is **a one-part "Reduced Score", not the orchestral score**, 294 measures, with a CC0 rights line.

No Haydn candidate combines original instrumentation, a confirmed file and a thorough analysis. The piano alternate is the Sonata Hob. XVI:49, first movement, whose analysis lists the key at each bar but no chords ([tonic-chord.com](https://tonic-chord.com/haydn-piano-sonata-in-eb-major-hob-xvi49-analysis/); 220-measure ASAP [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Haydn/Keyboard_Sonatas/49-1/xml_score.musicxml), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). The "Emperor" quartet variations have the cleanest score (CC0, original four parts, but all four movements in one 531-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/StringQuartets/main/scores/Haydn,_Joseph/String_Quartet_in_C_major,_Hob.III77,_Op.76_No.3/sq20428156.mxl)) and **no thorough analysis was found**. Hob. XVI:52 has an analysis that names individual chords ([tonic-chord.com](https://tonic-chord.com/haydn-piano-sonata-in-eb-major-hob-xvi52-analysis/)) and **no MusicXML was found**.

### Beethoven: no single thorough free analysis for any candidate

Score files are easy for Beethoven; the analysis side is not. **No free teacher-style analysis gives chord-by-chord harmony for a whole movement** of any candidate. What exists is a form-and-key outline with bar numbers, textbook figures that annotate one phrase, and expert chord-label files.

The **Pathétique second movement** is the best pairing: 73 bars, an outline that pins down the hard spots (the enharmonic modulations at bars 42 and 48) ([tonic-chord.com](https://tonic-chord.com/beethoven-piano-sonata-no-8-in-c-minor-pathetique-analysis/)), a textbook figure for bars 1 to 8 with phrase brackets and cadence labels ([Hutchinson, section 13.3](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/PeriodForm.html)), and a full expert label file of 78 lines ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Beethoven,_Ludwig_van/Op013%28Pathetique%29/2/analysis.txt)). Two files were confirmed at 73 measures: the ASAP file in the table and a MuseTrainer copy with no stated licence ([MuseTrainer file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Sonate_No._8_Pathetique_2nd_Movement.mxl)).

**Op. 2 No. 1, first movement**, is the only candidate with an annotated score of the whole movement: a six-page PDF by Ben Dunnett, a music teacher and examiner, with section labels printed on the score. It covers form only, with no Roman numerals ([Dunnett](https://www.musictheoryacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Beethoven-sonata-in-F-minor-sonata-form-annotated-sheet-music.pdf)). A bar-numbered outline ([tonic-chord.com](https://tonic-chord.com/beethoven-piano-sonata-no-1-in-f-minor-analysis/)), a textbook figure of bars 1 to 8 as a sentence ([Hutchinson, section 13.2](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/SentenceStructure.html)) and three separate expert label files ([When in Rome folder](https://github.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/tree/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Beethoven,_Ludwig_van/Op002_No1/1)) complete the set. The ASAP file has 153 measures and no repeat signs against 152 printed bars, so the upbeat is probably numbered as bar 1.

The alternates, in order: the Moonlight first movement has three prose sources that agree on the structure ([tonic-chord.com](https://tonic-chord.com/beethoven-piano-sonata-no-14-in-c-minor-analysis/)), but the fullest is anonymous and the only MusicXML has no licence and encodes the metre as 4/4 where the piece is in cut time ([69-measure file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Sonate_No._14_Moonlight_1st_Movement.mxl)). The Pathétique finale has the best textbook walk-through of a whole movement, eleven figures with keys and cadences in the prose ([Hutchinson, section 25.3](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/RondoForm.html); [ASAP file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Beethoven/Piano_Sonatas/8-3/xml_score.musicxml), 211 elements numbered to 210). Für Elise has four paragraphs of bar-referenced prose on Wikipedia ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCr_Elise)), no expert label file, and a [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Fur_Elise.mxl) whose 106 measures with repeats and endings will not match those bar numbers without a map. The Tempest, Waldstein and Appassionata first movements have files and label files but no teacher-style commentary was verified. Symphony No. 5 was found only as a piano arrangement. Tovey's bar-to-bar companion to the sonatas would probably upgrade any of these, but it is a restricted lending copy and was not opened ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/companiontobeeth0000dona)).

### Schubert: a manual label file stands in for the missing prose

For **"Der Lindenbaum"** the reference is Mark Gotham's manual Roman-numeral file: 104 lines with form labels (introduction, verses, episode, coda), mode mixture and augmented sixths, which are the Schubert features worth testing. It is a label file, not an explanation ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schubert,_Franz/Winterreise,_D.911/05_Der_Lindenbaum/analysis.txt)). The prose found is a master's thesis section (pages 28 to 36) that reads the piano part as narrative and never uses Roman numerals, and which mislabels the E minor stanza as the "relative minor" ([Dempsey, 2020](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=bgsu1579897972318386&disposition=inline)). The score is the cleanest of any slot: voice and piano, 82 measures, CC0.

The runner-up is "Gute Nacht": a 96-line manual label file ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schubert,_Franz/Winterreise,_D.911/01_Gute_Nacht/analysis.txt)), a Boston University worksheet with twelve bar-specific questions and no answer key ([Burton](https://people.bu.edu/burtond/resources/Teaching/5a8.401Handouts.pdf)), and a CC0 [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schubert,_Franz/Winterreise,_D.911/01_Gute_Nacht/score.mxl) of 105 measures whose repeat signs complicate bar numbering. "Erlkönig" has only a key-by-bar table ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlk%C3%B6nig_%28Schubert%29)) and no label file; its CC0 [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/Lieder/main/scores/Schubert,_Franz/_/Der_Erlk%C3%B6nig,_D.328/lc29062370.mxl) has 148 measures and names the voice part "Clarinet in C". "Ständchen" has a label file and a worksheet but no prose ([59-measure file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/OpenScore-LiederCorpus/Schubert,_Franz/Schwanengesang,_D.957/04_St%C3%A4ndchen/score.mxl)). For the Impromptu Op. 90 No. 3 only a thin blog post was found.

## Short teaching pieces carry the Romantic slots; Liszt and Grieg have no thorough analysis

### Chopin: Preludes 20 and 4, with the labels printed on the score

**Prelude No. 20 in C minor** is the strongest Chopin pairing. José Rodríguez Alvira's three pages on teoria.com print a Roman numeral under every chord for bars 1 to 4 and 5 to 8 (the last phrase repeats 5 to 8), covering the turn to A-flat, the half cadence, the passing diminished seventh, the augmented sixth and the Neapolitan ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2018/chopin-preludes/20/index.php)). A 1970 master's thesis on all 24 preludes gives a second treatment ([Daniel](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc663297/)), and a human label file gives a third ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Chopin%2C_Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric/Preludes%2C_Op.28/20/analysis.txt)). Three independent files agree on 13 measures; the alternates are the Tonal Piano Corpus engraving ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Chopin%2C%20Fryderyk%20%281810%20-%201849%29/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Op28/Pr%C3%A9ludeOp28No20.xml)) and a conversion of the Chopin Institute's first-edition score ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nereasastre/Music_Corpus_Annotator/main/public/craig_files/humdrum-chopin-first-editions-main/kern/028-1-BH-020.musicxml)).

**Prelude No. 4 in E minor** has the deepest teoria.com article: five pages (dated 2001, marked as peer reviewed by MERLOT) that derive bars 1 to 12 from a plain tonic, subdominant and dominant skeleton, graph the voice motion of bars 1 to 8, and give a Roman-numeral reduction of bars 17 to 25 including the deceptive cadence and the German sixth. It does not label phrases and cadences systematically and uses some jazz vocabulary ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/chopin-prelude-04/index.php)). This is the harder test of the two: a simple progression seen through heavy chromatic voice leading. Three files agree on 26 measure elements; the alternates are the Tonal Piano Corpus [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Chopin%2C%20Fryderyk%20%281810%20-%201849%29/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Op28/Pr%C3%A9ludeOp28No4.xml) and the first-edition conversion ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nereasastre/Music_Corpus_Annotator/main/public/craig_files/humdrum-chopin-first-editions-main/kern/028_1-12-1a-C-004.musicxml)).

The gentler alternate is Prelude No. 6 in B minor, whose two teoria.com images annotate the whole piece with chord symbols and Roman numerals ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2019/chopin-preludes/06/index.php); 26-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Chopin%2C%20Fryderyk%20%281810%20-%201849%29/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Op28/PreludeOp28No6.xml)). The Nocturne in E minor Op. 72 No. 1 has the best prose analysis of any Chopin candidate, an exam-board guide that walks through all 57 bars ([AQA guide](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/music/AQA-7272-TG-AOS1-CBG.PDF)), but **the only file found is a poor MIDI conversion** in two one-staff parts ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArturoBarrios/MusicClassification/master/MusicXML_Files/AmbroseTabsDatabase/doneClassical/NocturneNo19inEminor%288%29.musicxml)). The "Raindrop" Prelude has form, phrase and key coverage only ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/2019/chopin-preludes/15/index.php); 89-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nereasastre/Music_Corpus_Annotator/main/public/craig_files/humdrum-chopin-first-editions-main/kern/028-1-BH-015.musicxml)). For the **Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2 no thorough free analysis was found**: the best page is a teacher's form map that deliberately gives no chord-by-chord harmony ([Paperless Piano Teacher](https://paperlesspianoteacher.com/blog/chopin-nocturne-op-9-no-2-analysis)), and the standard scholarly analysis is a paywalled book chapter; the 38-element [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Chopin_-_Nocturne_Op_9_No_2_E_Flat_Major.mxl) is fine. The mazurkas have expert labels and files but no whole-piece teacher-style analysis.

### Schumann: Kinderszenen No. 1 has two independent analyses

**"Von fremden Ländern und Menschen"** has official Pearson Edexcel set-work notes (seven pages covering Nos. 1, 3 and 11, unsigned, with bar-and-beat references for structure, keys, every cadence, chromatic chords and suspensions, but not a chord for every beat; [Pearson Edexcel](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Music/2013/Teaching%20and%20learning%20materials/UNIT_3_-_23__Schumann_-_Kinderscenen_Op__15_-_Nos__1_3_and_11_June_2017.pdf)) and an independent analysis by Tom Sutcliffe, who holds a master's degree in music theory and analysis. Sutcliffe's two-page outline reduces all 22 bars to chords with Roman numerals, marks passing notes, auxiliary notes and the suspension, and draws a phrase tree; some of his labels follow his own root-progression theory and are non-standard ([formal analysis page](https://www.harmony.org.uk/book/schumann_analysis/schumann_formal_analysis.htm)). Two files agree on 22 measures. The Tonal Piano Corpus file in the table has no chord labels inside it and no licence. The alternative is a conversion of the DCML score (DCML is a research corpus of scores with chord labels made by expert annotators), licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which **contains 44 embedded expert chord labels that must be stripped first** ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/scores/schumann_kinderszenen/n01.musicxml)).

Alternates: Kinderszenen Nos. 11 and 3 use the same Pearson notes and have richer chromatic content ([No. 11 file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/scores/schumann_kinderszenen/n11.musicxml), 48 measures; [No. 3 file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/scores/schumann_kinderszenen/n03.musicxml), 32 elements). "Ich grolle nicht" has a CC0 voice-and-piano [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/Lieder/main/scores/Schumann%2C_Robert/Dichterliebe%2C_Op.48/07_Ich_grolle_nicht/lc4978382.mxl) of 36 measures, a human label file, and a bar-by-bar chord reading whose author calls himself an amateur ([Woltjer](https://geertwoltjer.nl/Songs/DichterliebeAnalysis/Dichterliebe7.html)). **Träumerei, the popular choice, has no free chord-level prose analysis**: the fullest text is a descriptive book excerpt that names keys and form but not chords ([Kapilow excerpt](https://www.maramarietta.com/schumann-kinderszenen-analysis-1-traumerei/)); it is workable only with the DCML labels ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/scores/schumann_kinderszenen/n07.musicxml), 25 elements, 76 embedded labels).

### Mendelssohn: the best analysis of the survey, and a file that needs repair

Jonathan Kotler's 2021 Rutgers PhD dissertation prints a complete annotated score of the **Song Without Words Op. 19 No. 1** in its Appendix A (pages 134 to 138): a Roman numeral or figure under every harmony, key-change labels, boxed cadence labels and phrase brackets. It does not cover non-chord tones, and its prose chapters concern the author's own quantitative measures, which are not needed here ([dissertation PDF](https://rucore.libraries.rutgers.edu/rutgers-lib/65438/PDF/1/play/)). This is the closest match to the tool's drawn output found anywhere. The file is the problem: the Tonal Piano Corpus XML has 62 measures but **is not valid UTF-8 and a standard XML parser rejects it** at the title line. It will probably load after re-saving as UTF-8, which was not tested. Kotler's score also shows a repeat with first and second endings at bar 15, while the file has no repeat marks, so bar numbers will need a map. A second file from a collection with known broken conversions has 54 measures and is low confidence ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArturoBarrios/MusicClassification/master/MusicXML_Files/ABRSM_2020_2021_Raw/01_18_b8_mendelssohn_song_without_words_op_19_no_1%287%29.musicxml)).

The better-known alternates are solid on the score side only. The Venetian Gondola Song Op. 30 No. 6 has two matching 56-measure files ([Tonal Piano Corpus](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Mendelssohn%2C%20Felix%20%281809%20-%201847%29/xml/Lieder%20ohne%20W%C3%B6rte/Op30No6.xml)), but its only substantial analysis is Schenker's essay in a paid book ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/49165/chapter/422055905)). The Spring Song Op. 62 No. 6 has a clean 90-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Mendelssohn%2C%20Felix%20%281809%20-%201847%29/xml/Lieder%20ohne%20W%C3%B6rte/Op62No6.xml) and only a form outline ([Liu](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/126014474.pdf)). Kotler also annotates Op. 19 Nos. 2, 3 and 4, for which no MusicXML was found.

### Brahms: Op. 118 No. 2 is the best-documented piece of the late-Romantic group

Four free sources together cover the **Intermezzo in A major** from different angles. Kelly Dean Hansen (PhD in musicology) gives a bar-numbered walk-through of all 116 bars for form, keys, cadences and texture, without Roman numerals ([Hansen](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/opus118.html)). A McGill coursework essay by Néstor Nápoles López treats formal functions and the cadence at bar 16 ([Nápoles](https://napulen.github.io/reports/mcgill/muth251/)). A 1979 master's thesis gives phrase lengths, Roman numerals for bars 1 to 8 and the pedal points (thesis pages 96 to 101) ([Yang](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc504293/m2/1/high_res_d/1002772369-Yang.pdf)). A theory professor's post covers the chromatic detail of bars 57 to 64 and says he has used the piece for years in his Theory III course ([Dahn](https://lukedahn.wordpress.com/2011/11/26/enharmonic-spellings-in-brahms-intermezzo-in-a-op-118-no-2/)). The ASAP file is numbered to 116 and matches Hansen. A Tonal Piano Corpus copy has 125 measure elements and will not match without adjustment ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Brahms%2C%20Johannes%20%281833%20-%201897%29/xml/Sechs%20Klavierst%C3%BCcke%20Op118/Op118No2.xml)).

Alternates: the Intermezzo Op. 117 No. 1 has two whole-piece analyses ([Hansen](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/opus117.html) and Yang) and a 58-element [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Brahms%2C%20Johannes%20%281833%20-%201897%29/xml/Drei%20Intermezzi%20Op117/Op117No1.xml) with no licence. The Wiegenlied has one analysis ([Hansen](http://www.kellydeanhansen.com/opus49.html)) and a CC0 [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/Lieder/main/scores/Brahms%2C_Johannes/5_Lieder%2C_Op.49/4_Wiegenlied/lc5701612.mxl) of 18 bars, but 18 bars over a tonic pedal gives little to check. Hungarian Dance No. 5 was found only as a solo-piano arrangement and no analysis was sought in depth.

### Tchaikovsky: "June" pairs a form article with expert labels

For **"June: Barcarolle"** a six-page open-access article by Shuaina Zhou and Chong Yew Yoong (2022) gives a form table by bar for the whole piece with keys; its harmonic remarks are sparse, about half of it is performance advice, and its table and text disagree slightly on section boundaries ([Zhou and Chong](https://article.bbwpublisher.com/uploads/file/files/journals/31/articles/4329/submission/proof/4329-313-12085-1-10-20220927.pdf)). The chord-level reference is the DCML expert label file, which labels every bar ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Tchaikovsky%2C_Pyotr/Seasons%2C_Op.37a/6/analysis.txt)). Two files have 99 measures, matching the article; the second is the Tonal Piano Corpus copy ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Tchaikovsky%2C%20Pyotr%20%281840%20-%201893%29/xml/The%20Seasons%20Op37/6%20Barcarolle.xml)). The other eleven months of The Seasons have the same labels and files but no prose analysis was found ([folder](https://github.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/tree/main/scores/tchaikovsky_seasons)). A paper on the harmony of the Album for Children exists but was not opened. The Swan Lake theme was found only as a short simplified arrangement, and no analysis was sought for the ballet numbers.

### Liszt and Grieg: plainly, no thorough analysis was found

**No Liszt piece has a thorough free analysis.** For Liebestraum No. 3, the popular choice, the one academic source analyses bars 37 to 41 only ([Mamedov, 2019](https://www.ijiet.org/vol9/1273-E127.pdf)); the 88-element [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/scores/Liebestraum_No._3_in_A_Major.mxl) looks complete and has no stated licence. A long blog analysis of it was set aside because no human author could be established. Sonetto 104 del Petrarca is the more checkable piece: it has expert DCML chord labels for the whole piece and a 1975 master's thesis that compares Liszt's four versions with examples, though not bar by bar ([Van der Merwe](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc663042/m2/1/high_res_d/1002773327-Van_der_Merwe.pdf)). Consolation No. 3 is discussed in two scholarly books that were not opened, and **no MusicXML was found for it**. La Campanella has files and no analysis was sought; Un sospiro has neither.

**No Grieg piece has a thorough free analysis either.** The Notturno Op. 54 No. 4 has two verified 63-measure files and DCML expert labels, but the prose is a teaching post about rhythm ([Williamson](https://www.jannawilliamson.com/blog/how-to-teach-grieg-notturno)), a book that was not opened and a video that was not watched. The Arietta Op. 12 No. 1 has a 23-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/scores/grieg_lyric_pieces/op12n01.musicxml), labels, and a tutorial that treats harmony for the first two lines only ([PianoTV](https://www.pianotv.net/2019/03/griegs-arietta-tutorial-an-intermediate-step-to-liszt/)). Wedding Day at Troldhaugen has a 179-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/scores/grieg_lyric_pieces/op65n06.musicxml) and labels. Morning Mood was found only as a 16-bar simplification. Both slots become workable only if the owner accepts expert chord labels, which are human-made but carry no explanation, as the main reference.

## Debussy is covered bar by bar; Ravel and Rachmaninoff are not

### Debussy: two preludes with harmony named at each step

**"La fille aux cheveux de lin"** has the analysis closest to a teacher's: Gonzalo Varela wrote it as a marking key while a harmony teaching assistant at the Universidad de la República in Montevideo. Its four pages of text go through all 39 bars in 18 bar-range entries, naming chords and Roman numerals, pentatonic and hexatonic fields, parallel chords, cadence types and the modulations, and taking positions on ambiguous spots ([Varela](https://gonzalovarela.com/assets/doc/studies/An%C3%A1lisis%20arm%C3%B3nico%20de%20%27La%20fille%20aux%20cheveux%20de%20lin%27%20%28Preludio%20Libro%201%20No.%208%29%20de%20Claude%20Debussy-EN.pdf)). Two independent analyses allow cross-checks: a topic-by-topic one on teoria.com ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/debussy-fille-aux-cheveux-de-lin/index.php)) and a Schenkerian journal article ([Day-O'Connell](https://www.skidmore.edu/music/documents/Day-OConnell_Debussy_Pentatonicism.pdf)). The file has 39 bars.

**"Des pas sur la neige"** is recommended second because its teoria.com analysis (2011) also proceeds in bar order with the harmony named at each step: five pages covering each reharmonisation of the ostinato, chord extensions, named modes, the whole-tone bars 14 to 15 and the final cadence, with one score image per bar range ([teoria.com](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/debussy-des-pas-sur-la-neige/index.php)). It does not cover phrase structure. The file has 36 bars, short enough to check by hand.

The alternates test different things. "Voiles" and "La cathédrale engloutie" are both analysed whole in a 2017 master's thesis, with form diagrams and exact region boundaries (for "Voiles": whole-tone bars 1 to 41, pentatonic 42 to 47, whole-tone 48 to 64), but no chord labels or cadences ([Loyd](https://mospace.umsystem.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/b2825388-3053-43fa-81f8-15f8c91e278b/content)). The researcher's own suggestion was to pair "La fille" with "Voiles" for variety; "Voiles" is the right swap if naming scale regions matters more than naming chords. Its 64-bar [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/esthy13/memuk/main/melody/partitions/ATEPP-1.1/raw/Claude_Debussy/Pr%C3%A9ludes%2C_Book_1%2C_L._117/No._2%2C_Voiles/Debussy_-_Voiles.mxl) came through a third-party mirror and has no title metadata; the "Cathédrale" [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Debussy%2C%20Claude%20%281862%20-%201914%29/xml/Pr%C3%A9ludes%20Book1/10%20La%20Cath%C3%A9drale%20engloutie.xml) has 89 bars. **For "Clair de lune" no teacher-style analysis was read**: there is a complete expert label file ([label file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Keyboard_Other/Debussy,_Claude/Suite_Bergamasque,_L.75/3_Clair/analysis.txt)) and a 72-bar [file](https://musetrainer.github.io/library/scores/Clair_de_Lune__Debussy.mxl), but the one page titled as a complete analysis could not be opened. Arabesque No. 1 has a 107-bar file and no verified analysis.

### Ravel: two doctoral analyses, neither chord by chord

The search did not find enough solid Ravel candidates; the two below are the only ones whose analysis was read directly. For the **Sonatine, first movement**, Robert Hjelmstad's 2019 doctoral thesis gives the sonata-form outline with bar numbers, the key areas and thirteen music examples; most of it is motivic, so it checks form and tonal centres more than chords ([Hjelmstad](https://scholar.colorado.edu/downloads/xg94hp86p)). The file contains the whole movement but **writes the exposition repeat out**: for any analysis bar of 26 or higher, the file bar is that number plus 25 (the recapitulation Hjelmstad puts at bar 56 is at file bar 81).

For **"Jeux d'eau"**, Ting-Yu Liu's 2023 doctoral document (pages 20 to 28) names themes, chords and pentatonic, whole-tone, octatonic and polytonal passages at about fourteen bar locations, so it confirms specific events but not a continuous reading ([Liu](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/1c78b6ba-97fe-4d56-90fb-15995c420e7b/content)). The file is large (88 measures, three staves, 4,740 notes) and was reached through a mirror of the ATEPP dataset, not the official archive. Liu's highest bar reference is 84 for "the last few measures", so numbering around the cadenza is unresolved. A master's thesis in music theory on the same piece exists but its PDF could not be opened ([Penn State record](https://etda.libraries.psu.edu/catalog/15275exd42)).

The Pavane pour une infante défunte would be the more useful second piece musically, and it has the most reliable Ravel file (ASAP, 72 bars, [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Ravel/Pavane/xml_score.musicxml)), but **no thorough Pavane analysis was read**: what exists is a student presentation outline ([Rice](https://prezi.com/ggmelzxludtz/pavane-pour-une-infante-defunte/)), a blog post without consistent bar numbers, and a one-line key scheme. The Menuet sur le nom d'Haydn has a 550-word blog analysis and a [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Ravel%2C%20Maurice%20%281875%20-%201937%29/xml/Menuet%20Haydn.xml) with 55 bars where the blog says 54. The String Quartet has a CC0 file of all movements ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenScore/StringQuartets/main/scores/Ravel%2C_Maurice/String_Quartet_in_F_major/sq8482283.mxl)) and no analysis that could be opened. No MusicXML was found for the Menuet from Le Tombeau de Couperin, Ma mère l'Oye or Boléro.

### Rachmaninoff: the deep analysis and the famous piece are different preludes

**No source gives a full Roman-numeral analysis of any Rachmaninoff piece.** The slot that can run today is the **Prelude in B minor Op. 32 No. 10**: Robert Buxton's 115-page doctoral dissertation (2018, 65 music examples) covers the whole piece for rhythm, phrase structure, form and the two climaxes, and names harmony at key moments, such as the Neapolitan C major chord at the peak in bar 34. Its framing is philosophical, so the harmonic remarks serve that argument ([Buxton](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1248480/m2/1/high_res_d/BUXTON-DISSERTATION-2018.pdf)). Two files were confirmed: the ASAP file in the table and a 63-measure Tonal Piano Corpus copy ([file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Rachmaninov%2C%20Sergey%20%281873%20-%201943%29/xml/Preludes%20Op32/PreludeOp32No10.xml)).

The **Prelude in C-sharp minor Op. 3 No. 2** is the best match of fame and analysis quality. Karen Bottge's peer-reviewed article gives voice-leading reductions of bars 1 to 14, 14 to 26 and 27 to 42 and reads the Agitato as a sentence, though about half the article is about Adorno and reception, and the reprise gets little harmonic discussion ([Bottge](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.11.17.4/mto.11.17.4.bottge.html)). **No MusicXML was confirmed.** A MuseScore file is listed on IMSLP but the download returned a bot-check page, and it would then need exporting to MusicXML ([IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Morceaux_de_fantaisie,_Op.3_%28Rachmaninoff,_Sergei%29)); Mutopia has a LilyPond source under CC BY-SA 4.0 but no MusicXML ([Mutopia](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=2033)). A person fetching the IMSLP file by hand would close this gap.

The fallback is the Prelude in G minor Op. 23 No. 5, with a verified 86-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Rachmaninov%2C%20Sergey%20%281873%20-%201943%29/xml/Preludes%20Op23/PreludeOp23No5.xml) and only a thin outline with bar ranges and two cadences ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prelude_in_G_minor_%28Rachmaninoff%29)). The Prelude in D major Op. 23 No. 4 is thinner still ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prelude_in_D_major_%28Rachmaninoff%29); 77-measure [file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/Rachmaninoff/Preludes_op_23/4/xml_score.musicxml)). The Vocalise is weak on both sides (an unwatched video; a cello arrangement in E minor that could not be downloaded), and the Élégie has a file and no analysis. All shortlisted Rachmaninoff pieces were published between 1893 and 1915 and are in the public domain in the United States and in life-plus-70 countries ([Duke, Public Domain Day 2026](https://web.law.duke.edu/cspd/publicdomainday/2026/)); the Paganini Rhapsody of 1934 is still under copyright in the United States ([IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Rhapsody_on_a_Theme_of_Paganini,_Op.43_%28Rachmaninoff,_Sergei%29)).

## Bar maps, licences and embedded answers need handling before any run

**Bar numbering.** A measure count in a file is not always the bar count an analysis uses, because pickups and first and second endings are stored as separate measures and some files write repeats out. The cases found: BWV 269 (24 elements, numbered to 21); the music21 copy of BWV 846 (34 against 35); Beethoven Op. 2 No. 1 (153 against 152, and one label file numbers the upbeat as bar 0); Mozart K. 333 and K. 331 (a few extra elements); Chopin Prelude No. 4 (26 elements for 25 bars); Mendelssohn Op. 19 No. 1 (repeat written out); Ravel Sonatine (add 25 from bar 26); Brahms Op. 118 No. 2 in the Tonal Piano Corpus copy (125 against 116); "Gute Nacht" and Für Elise (repeats and endings); the Menuet sur le nom d'Haydn (55 against 54); and "Jeux d'eau" (88 measures against a highest cited bar of 84). One analysis source also numbers differently from the score: the teoria.com tutorial table for the BWV 847 fugue runs to bar 32 and places entries a bar later than Bruhn and the 31-measure files, so it should not be used for bar-level checks ([teoria.com tutorial](https://www.teoria.com/en/tutorials/forms/contrapuntal/05-episodes.php)). Each pair needs its numbering checked at the first bar before any comparison.

**Licences.** The compositions are all public domain; each digital engraving has its own terms. Clearly free to redistribute: the OpenScore songs and quartets (CC0) ([OpenScore Lieder](https://github.com/OpenScore/Lieder)), the Haydn 104 reduction (CC0), and the Bach prelude files in When in Rome (CC BY-SA 4.0). Non-commercial only: ASAP ([ASAP README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/master/README.md)), the DCML corpora and their MusicXML conversions ([piano_corpora_dcml README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fosfrancesco/piano_corpora_dcml/main/README.md)). The Mozart sonata files in When in Rome are conversions of DCML files, so they most likely carry DCML's non-commercial terms despite the repository's CC BY-SA badge; the files themselves contain no rights line ([remote.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome/master/Corpus/Piano_Sonatas/Mozart,_Wolfgang_Amadeus/K332/1/remote.json)). No licence at all: the Tonal Piano Corpus, which supplies five of the recommended files ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hectorbellmann-art/Tonal-Piano-Corpus/main/Readme.md)); MuseTrainer, whose README calls it a public domain library without per-file provenance ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/musetrainer/library/master/README.md)); and the files for the BWV 847 fugue and the Inventions. All of these are fine for an internal check. Only the CC0 and CC BY-SA files are clearly safe to ship as examples. The published analyses are also under copyright: they can serve as a checking reference, but their text and figures should not be copied into the product.

**Files that contain the answer.** The MusicXML conversions of DCML scores carry the expert chord labels inside the file as harmony elements. This was confirmed for Kinderszenen No. 1 (44 labels), Träumerei (76) and the mazurkas, and was not checked for the Tchaikovsky, Grieg and Liszt files from the same conversion, which should be assumed to have them. They must be stripped before a run so they neither print on the engraved score nor give the tool the answer. Separately, some corpus folders hold a file named analysis_automatic.rntxt, which is machine output and must not be used as the human reference ([When in Rome repository](https://github.com/MarkGotham/When-in-Rome)).

**Arrangements, reductions and transpositions.** The Haydn 104 file is a reduction. Found only as arrangements or simplifications, and so not recommended: Bach's Air and Toccata and Fugue in D minor, Beethoven's Symphony No. 5, Brahms's Hungarian Dance No. 5, Tchaikovsky's Swan Lake theme, Grieg's Morning Mood, Schumann's "Wild Rider" (a lead sheet), Rachmaninoff's Vocalise (a cello version in E minor) and Bach's BWV 999 (in D minor).

**Files that failed or look wrong.** The Mendelssohn Op. 19 No. 1 file fails to parse. The Tonal Piano Corpus files declare a very old MusicXML version (0.7b, a Finale 2004 export), so the tool's loader should be tried on one before relying on this source. The same corpus is a single author's repository created in May 2026, gives Debussy's dates as 1862 to 1914, labels its Menuet antique as an excerpt, and has a Pavane with no 2/4 bars where the ASAP file has them at bars 19, 49 and 59. Files from the "ABRSM raw" and "AmbroseTabs" collections are broken or MIDI-derived (Chopin Prelude No. 6 with 43 measures in 4/4, "Voiles" with 77 measures, the Spring Song in G major) and should not be used ([repository](https://github.com/ArturoBarrios/MusicClassification)).

**Different notation and honest disagreement.** Martin writes all numerals in upper case; the British exam-board documents write Ib and Vc for inversions and say perfect, imperfect and interrupted cadence; Sutcliffe and the teoria.com articles each have a personal slant. These need translating before a label-for-label comparison. Human analysts also disagree with each other: at bar 12 of BWV 269 the professor reads an apparent I6 standing for the dominant where the label file has I6 then V7; three sources count the entries and stretti of the BWV 846 fugue differently; and two sources split over where the transition of K. 545 begins. A difference from one source is not by itself a tool error.

### What could not be verified

No file was rendered, played or proofread against a printed edition, and none was run through the tool, so note accuracy and bar-number behaviour in the engine are untested. Several annotated images were not seen: the Puget Sound fugue figures could not be rendered, the two score pages at the end of Varela's PDF could not be viewed, and only a sample of the teoria.com images was looked at. Three sources were read only through a summarising fetch tool and their wording is unconfirmed: the Open University course on K. 545 and K. 333, the Kapilow excerpt on Träumerei, and all the Pavane material. The credentials of José Rodríguez Alvira (teoria.com), Clark Ross and Richard Bruner were not checked against an independent page, and the tonic-chord.com pages name no author or source. No printed textbook was checked (Caplin, Laitz, Aldwell and Schachter, Burkhart's anthology, Tovey), so no chapter or page numbers can be given. A number of theses sit behind bot checks and were not read, including ones on the Rachmaninoff preludes, Tchaikovsky's Seasons, Liszt's Petrarch sonnets, Chopin's Prelude No. 4 and Mendelssohn's Songs Without Words. IMSLP downloads were blocked, musescore.com was not used because it needs a login, the KernScores server was down, and converting DCML's MuseScore files to MusicXML was not tested. Popularity was judged informally throughout.

## Conclusion

The search shows that "analysed the way a teacher would" and "popular" overlap less than the slot list assumes. Label-on-the-score analyses exist almost only for pieces under about 65 bars, so the cleanest evidence of the tool's chord accuracy will come from the Bach chorale, the two Chopin preludes, Kinderszenen No. 1, "La fille" and, once its file is repaired, Mendelssohn Op. 19 No. 1. For everything longer, the check splits into two separate questions with separate references: does the tool's form, key and cadence reading agree with the prose, and do its individual numerals agree with the expert label file. Planning the test that way makes the Beethoven, Haydn, Schubert and Tchaikovsky slots useful even though no single source covers them fully.

Three owner decisions would settle most of what is open. First, whether expert chord labels without prose are acceptable as the reference; if so, Liszt's Sonetto 104 and Grieg's Notturno become workable and the famous alternates (Träumerei, Clair de lune) come into reach. Second, whether fame or checkability wins where they pull apart, as with Rachmaninoff's C-sharp minor and G minor preludes against Op. 32 No. 10. Third, whether unlicensed files are acceptable for internal testing. The cheapest upgrades need a person, not more searching: fetch the Rachmaninoff file from IMSLP in a browser, re-save the Mendelssohn file as UTF-8, convert DCML's MuseScore files, and open the blocked theses or borrow Tovey for the Beethoven sonatas.
