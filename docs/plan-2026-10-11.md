# Plan: from a finished v1 to a product people use (2026-10-11)

This is a handoff plan. The agent picking it up has not seen the earlier sessions. Read this file, then `docs/plan.md` (the approved v1 plan), the last ten entries of `docs/engineering-log.md`, and `README.md`, in that order. The market research that this plan rests on is summarised in the appendix with its sources.

## 1. Where the product stands

**What it is.** A harmonic analysis drawn on the engraved score, for use inside Claude (plugin + skill + connector) and ChatGPT (MCP server). The user attaches a MusicXML score, names a passage of up to 32 bars, and gets back the page as PNG and vector PDF with Roman numerals, function colours, cadences, phrase brackets, non-chord tones, voice-leading arrows and numbered callouts, plus a commentary pitched to a student or a composer. Three views (harmony, voice leading, form) and a piano reduction for scores with more than four staves.

**How it works.** AnalysisGNN drafts the analysis on the server (`engine/draft.py`, `server/app.py`); the chat model reviews the draft against a beat-by-beat note table (`skill/SKILL.md`, method section) and writes an annotation list (`docs/annotation-spec.md`); the engine engraves it with Verovio and draws the marks (`engine/`). In Claude the skill runs the engine in the sandbox and sends only a compact note list to the server, because Claude connectors cannot receive attachments. In ChatGPT the server does everything.

**Quality.** 71% full agreement with expert chord labels on 12 pieces, against about 78% expert-to-expert. Weak spots, stated in the listing text: borrowed chords read as a key change; impressionist harmony gets looser descriptions. Accuracy work was paused by the owner on 2026-10-04; do not reopen it without being asked (see §3).

**Cost and limits.** Cloud Run, one instance at most, daily caps of 100 drafts and 200 renders (`server/app.py`, `DAILY_LIMITS`), owner's budget $5 a month. Site on Cloudflare at sheetmusicanalysis.com.

**Status on 2026-10-11.** Server revision 18 deployed 2026-10-10 with the vector PDF and the piano reduction. Listing text is drafted in `docs/listing.md`. The engineering log has no record of the Claude directory or ChatGPT submissions having been sent, and there are no usage numbers yet. Confirm with the owner before assuming either way.

**Scope that is settled.** v1 is classical, Bach to early twentieth century. Jazz and pop are v2. The name, domain and look are final. The owner does not want benchmarking against other AI analysis tools; the accuracy reference is human teacher or textbook analyses.

## 2. What the research says people want

Full findings and sources are in the appendix. The short form:

1. **The core offer has no competition in its exact form.** Forum users have asked for "an analysis printed on the score" and been told nothing does it. Dorico has had an open request for Roman numerals since 2017. MuseScore's only numeral plugin ignores the bass and cannot find the key. Nobody engraves the analysis.
2. **Most people do not have MusicXML.** They have PDFs, photos or MIDI. The MusicXML-only gate is the largest loss of users before they see a page. Born-digital PDFs convert well with current optical music recognition (OMR); scans and photos convert badly.
3. **Raw chatbots are publicly known to fail at reading scores**, and to do far better when given structured notation. This is our pitch, and there are citable 2025 and 2026 sources for it.
4. **Checking is as wanted as analysing.** Teachers find grading Roman-numeral homework tedious. Four-part-writing checkers are paid features elsewhere. No chat tool offers "check my analysis".
5. **Exam syllabi are a concrete audience** (ABRSM grades 6 to 8, RCM, AP Music Theory), each with its own notation conventions.
6. **Store discovery is poor.** The GPT store is a small share of ChatGPT traffic; skill directories show single-digit installs. This audience reads Scoring Notes and the Steinberg and MuseScore forums, and finds things by web search.
7. **Price points of comparable tools:** $5 to $10 a month or about $50 a year; pay-per-use credits are well received. Not needed now; recorded for a later decision.

## 3. Rules for this work

These come from the owner's instructions and from decisions recorded in earlier sessions. They are not optional.

- **Ship step by step.** One phase at a time, each deployed and checked before the next. Do not start a phase that depends on an owner decision until the decision is in.
- **Show before building any display.** For every new mark on the page or new kind of reply, produce real examples in Claude and in ChatGPT and show them to the owner before building the full path. The owner decides how it looks.
- **Labels must never be cramped.** Any new mark competes with existing ones; the engraving check (`tools/engraving_check.py`) must stay clean. Other elements yield to label text.
- **No workaround flows.** No codes to paste, no "go to this page and come back". If a platform cannot do something, say so plainly to the user and find a route that feels native. (The history: an upload page with a code, and a file button for an already-attached file, were both rejected.)
- **Measure cost before deploying anything that adds Cloud spend.** Time and memory per call, the price beyond the free tier, and the daily cap it needs. Quote it to the owner. Deploys go through `server/deploy.sh` and an Opus subagent at medium effort.
- **Keep evaluation usage low.** Two or three pieces, no repeats, free deterministic checks before any paid model run. Quote the cost of any paid run first.
- **Public text is for beginners.** Site and listing text must read for someone with very basic music knowledge. No personal name on the site.
- **Record each challenge in `docs/engineering-log.md`** with before and after numbers, in the same style as entries 1 to 29.
- **Do not reopen** accuracy work, the name, or comparisons with other AI tools. Do not email the AnalysisGNN author.
- **Outward actions are the owner's.** Directory submissions, forum posts, and anything that publishes text under the product's name are prepared by the agent and sent by the owner.

## 4. Phases

Each phase lists the goal, the steps, the files, the check that it is done, and the decisions that belong to the owner. Order matters: phase 1 before anything else, then 2, and 3 to 6 in the order the owner prefers once usage data exists.

### Phase 1. Get users (first)

Nothing below is worth building without people using the tool. This phase is mostly owner actions with the agent preparing everything.

Steps:

1. Confirm with the owner whether the Claude directory (plugin + connector at claude.ai/directory/manage) and ChatGPT (platform.openai.com/plugins) submissions have been sent. If not, walk through `docs/listing.md` field by field with the owner and make the carousel screenshots it specifies (four prompts, response only, PNG at least 1000 px wide), using the live plugin and a public-domain score. Put the screenshots in `out/listing/` and attach them to the log.
2. Write a short "launch" entry in the log once each listing is accepted, with the date and the final listing URLs.
3. Prepare, for the owner to post, three forum replies that answer existing threads where the demand is stated, in the thread's own terms and without marketing language: the MuseScore "analysis printed on the score" thread, the Steinberg Roman-numeral threads, and one Hooktheory or VI-Control thread about voice leading. Each reply: what the tool does, its limits (MusicXML only, classical, the known weak spots), and a link to the gallery. Draft them in `docs/outreach-2026-10.md`. The owner posts or does not.
4. Prepare a one-paragraph pitch to Scoring Notes for the owner to send, with two gallery pages attached.
5. Add a usage summary so the next decisions rest on data: a script `tools/usage_report.py` that reads the server's per-call log (the `log(...)` calls in `server/app.py`; check where they land on Cloud Run, probably Cloud Logging) and prints, per day and per host: drafts, renders, piano renders, views asked for, limit hits, errors, and the measure ranges requested. No new cloud cost: read the logs that already exist. If the log lines lack a field the report needs (for example the view), add it to the log call.

Done when: both listings are live or the owner has decided to delay one; the outreach drafts exist; the usage report runs against the live logs.

Owner decisions: send or not send each submission; post or not post each forum reply.

### Phase 2. Accept PDFs

The biggest funnel loss. Already named as v2 in `docs/plan.md` ("homr or Audiveris with a confirm-the-score step"). Scope it to born-digital PDFs first; scans and photos are explicitly a later step and the user must be told so plainly, not left to fail.

Steps:

1. **Spike, before any design.** On three born-digital PDFs (a Bach chorale, a Mozart sonata movement, a Chopin prelude; make them with MuseScore from the `.mxl` files already used in tests so ground truth exists) and two scanned PDFs from IMSLP, run two OMR tools and compare the MusicXML they produce with the ground truth: note count, pitch agreement per bar, measure count, and whether the engine's `ScoreIndex` can read the result at all. Candidates: `homr` (Python, ML, pip-installable; check its model download size and whether it runs on CPU in under 30 s a page) and Audiveris (Java, mature, heavy). Write the numbers in the log. Decide on one tool from the numbers, or on none if neither clears about 95% pitch agreement on clean PDFs.
2. **Decide where OMR runs, per host.** In ChatGPT the server already has the file, so the server runs it. In Claude the file is in the sandbox and cannot reach the server as an attachment; the skill would run OMR in the sandbox (pip install plus model download on first use, as it already does for music21) or the sandbox would send the page images to the server. Measure both: sandbox install time and memory, and server time and memory per page. The deciding rule is "no workaround flows": whatever the user sees must be attach, ask, answer.
3. **Cost gate.** Quote the server cost per PDF page at the current Cloud Run settings and propose a daily cap for OMR runs (a new key in `DAILY_LIMITS`) that keeps the month inside the $5 budget. Wait for the owner.
4. **Confirm-the-score step, shown to the owner first.** After OMR, engrave the recognised score with the existing `engrave_score` path and show it to the user with the sentence "This is what I read from your PDF; check it against your copy before I analyse it", then analyse on their go-ahead. Produce this in both hosts for one piece and show the owner before wiring the rest.
5. Implement: a `read_pdf` stage in front of the existing pipeline (skill script and server tool), the new cap, the log entry, the site text in `site/docs.html` (replace "PDFs, photos of printed music and audio are not supported yet" with what is and is not supported, written for beginners), the listing text, and the skill description so Claude triggers on an attached PDF.
6. Add two PDFs to `tools/robustness.py` so the stage is covered by the robustness run.

Done when: a born-digital PDF of a test piece goes from attachment to analysed page in both hosts, the robustness run passes, the cost is in the log and under the cap, and the site says what works.

Owner decisions: which OMR tool; where it runs in Claude; the cap; the wording of the confirm step; whether scans are offered at all in this phase.

### Phase 3. "Check my analysis" mode

Turns the known accuracy ceiling into a feature: the tool is a second reader, not an answer key. Students doing ABRSM, RCM or college homework are the audience.

Steps:

1. **Input forms.** Two: the student's labels typed in the chat ("bar 1 I, bar 2 V6, ..."), and labels already in the MusicXML (MuseScore's Roman numeral analysis text is exported as `<harmony>` or as lyrics; check which, in MuseScore 4's export, on one file). Parse both into the same `bar.beat → label` list in `engine/`.
2. **Comparison.** Reuse the comparison logic in `eval/run.py` (key, root, whole chord, chord with bass) to score the student's labels against the reviewed analysis, beat by beat. Classify each difference: same chord different spelling (not an error), different inversion, different chord, different key.
3. **Display, shown to the owner first.** Proposals to put in front of the owner as real pages: a tick or a small mark beside agreeing numerals, the tool's reading beneath the student's where they differ, and callouts only for the differences that matter. Add whatever is chosen to `docs/annotation-spec.md` and the renderer. Keep labels uncramped; the engraving check must stay clean.
4. **Commentary.** In the skill method: when checking, explain each difference in terms of the notes (what the bass is, which notes are present), say when both readings are defensible, and never claim certainty where the tool's own weak spots apply. Add the same text to the server-side method sent to ChatGPT.
5. **Framing text** on the site and in the listing: for learning, not for submitting; some courses forbid AI help and the student should check their course rules. Written for beginners.

Done when: a student's typed labels for 8 bars of a test piece produce a page and commentary in both hosts that the owner has approved; a labelled MusicXML does the same; unit of cost is unchanged (no new model runs beyond the existing draft and render).

Owner decisions: the marks on the page; the framing text.

### Phase 4. Voice-leading error report

For chorale and SATB uploads, which students write and teachers grade. Rules-based, so it is deterministic and free to run.

Steps:

1. Implement checks in a new `engine/part_writing.py` using music21's voice-leading objects on the existing score (music21 is already an optional import for the reduction): parallel fifths and octaves, direct fifths and octaves in outer voices, doubled leading tone, unresolved leading tone, unresolved chordal seventh, voice crossing and overlap, spacing over an octave between upper voices, range per voice. Each result: bars, beats, voices, the rule, one sentence of why.
2. Only offer the report for scores with four parts or fewer and a chorale-like texture; detect that from the note table.
3. Display, shown to the owner first: the existing voice-leading arrow and callout marks probably suffice (an arrow on the offending pair, a numbered callout explained in the commentary). Do not add a new mark unless the pages show it is needed.
4. Test on three Bach chorales from the robustness corpus (expect few or no errors, and read every hit by hand) and on two deliberately wrong SATB exercises written for the test (expect every planted error found). Put both in `tests/`.
5. Skill and server: a `--check-voice-leading` option in the render path and a line in the method about when to offer it.

Done when: planted errors are found, Bach chorales produce no false reports that a teacher would dispute, and the page is approved.

Owner decisions: the display; whether the report is offered by default on chorale textures or only on request.

### Phase 5. Notation presets

Cheap, and opens the exam-prep audience. Three styles for the same analysis:

- **Figured bass** under the bass staff alongside the numerals (Piston style), as asked for on the Steinberg forum.
- **ABRSM letters** for inversions (Ib, Vc) instead of figures, as the ABRSM grades 6 to 8 syllabus uses.
- **The current style** stays the default.

Steps: a `style` field on the annotation list (`docs/annotation-spec.md`), handled in the numeral renderer; a skill and server argument; three gallery pages, one per style, on the site; a sentence in the skill method so the model picks the ABRSM style when the user mentions ABRSM or a grade. The engraving check runs for each style. Show the three pages to the owner before publishing.

Done when: the same 8 bars render in all three styles with no label crowding and the owner has approved the pages.

### Phase 6. Export the analysis back into the editor

The oldest open request on the Steinberg forum is Roman numerals inside Dorico; MuseScore users want them in MuseScore. Give the user a MusicXML with the analysis embedded, next to the PNG and PDF.

Steps:

1. Spike first: write numerals into a MusicXML as `<harmony>` with `<numeral>` (MusicXML 4.0) and, separately, as a lyric line under the bass; import each into MuseScore 4 and Dorico 5 and see what survives. Record it in the log. If neither host shows the numerals usefully, stop here and say so.
2. Implement the one that works in `engine/` as an export next to the PDF, offered in the reply as a download.
3. Site text: what the export is, which editors it was checked in.

Done when: a user can open the exported file in MuseScore 4 and see the numerals under the right bars.

### Not in this plan

Jazz and pop lead sheets (real demand, off the classical positioning; still v2 after this plan). Scans and photos as input (after phase 2 proves clean PDFs). MIDI and audio input. Pricing and accounts (no usage data yet; the price points in §2 are for a later decision). A mobile app. A MuseScore or Dorico plugin (their plugin APIs change often and would be a second product to maintain).

## 5. How to report back to the owner

At the end of each phase: what was built, the before and after numbers, the cost quote, the open owner decisions, each as a short list. Show pages as images. Keep public text to beginner level. Do not say a phase is done until the check in its "Done when" line has actually run; if it could not run, say NOT RUN.
